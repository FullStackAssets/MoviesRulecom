Alan Ritchson may be on everyone’s radar as the new Jack Reacher, but his résumé includes a brief but memorable turn in one of the most iconic sci‑fi franchises of the past decade. In 2013’s The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, he appears as Gloss, a Career district tribute forced back into the arena by the Quarter Quell. Though his screen time is limited, the role placed him alongside the franchise’s core cast and introduced him to a global fan base. Understanding this early credit helps fans see the trajectory that led to his Reacher casting. It also highlights how even small parts in blockbuster series can boost an actor’s visibility.

## What happened
In The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, Ritchson portrays Gloss, a Victor from the Career district who, along with his sister Cashmere, is re‑aped for the Quarter Quell arena. The film depicts the tributes’ forced return to the Games despite their previous victory, underscoring the Capitol’s ruthless control.

Gloss’s dialogue is sparse, but his presence is felt during the training sequences and the arena’s early confrontations, sharing screen space with Katniss Everdeen (Jennifer Lawrence) and Peeta Mellark (Josh Hutcherson). The character ultimately meets the same tragic fate as many tributes, reinforcing the story’s themes of sacrifice.

## Why it matters
Ritchson’s participation links him to a franchise that defined a generation of YA sci‑fi adaptations, giving him exposure to a massive, dedicated fan community. That visibility likely contributed to casting directors noticing his physicality and screen presence, factors that later secured him the lead in Prime Video’s Reacher. The role also illustrates how actors can leverage even minor parts in high‑profile projects to build momentum toward larger opportunities.
