Chris Pratt has publicly expressed his enthusiasm for seeing director James Gunn return to the Marvel Cinematic Universe for a fourth Guardians of the Galaxy film. The comment came shortly after Gunn announced his departure from DC Studios, where he had spent four years leading the new franchise. Pratt’s remarks underscore the strong creative chemistry that helped shape the Guardians trilogy, which collectively earned nearly $2.5 billion worldwide. Fans and industry watchers see this as a potential catalyst for Marvel’s future space‑adventure slate. The actor’s endorsement adds weight to ongoing speculation about the franchise’s next chapter.

<Callout type="takeaway">Pratt’s support signals that a Gunn‑directed Guardians sequel remains a fan‑favorite possibility.</Callout>

## What happened
Chris Pratt told MovieZine that he would “love” to see James Gunn back behind the camera for a fourth Guardians of the Galaxy installment, praising Gunn as “the man” and noting that the director handled all three previous films, a rarity in modern franchises. Pratt added that he hasn’t spoken with Gunn since the news of the DC exit broke, but the prospect of a reunion excites him.

Gunn’s recent exit from DC Studios follows a four‑year tenure that ended with Skydance’s acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. He and co‑head Peter Safran will step down from their leadership roles on Dec. 1, making way for Marvel veteran Jeremy Latcham. Both Gunn and Safran will return to full‑time creative work, though their exact future projects remain unclear.

The Guardians of the Galaxy trilogy, directed by Gunn and starring Pratt as Star‑Lord, amassed nearly $2.5 billion worldwide, cementing its status as one of Marvel’s most lucrative space‑opera franchises.

## Why it matters
Gunn’s distinctive tone—mixing irreverent humor with heartfelt moments—has become synonymous with the Guardians brand, and his return could preserve that identity for a fourth film. For Marvel, securing Gunn would provide continuity and a proven box‑office engine, potentially smoothing the integration of new characters and story arcs. Conversely, without an official commitment, the franchise risks a creative vacuum that could dilute its momentum in an increasingly crowded MCU slate.
