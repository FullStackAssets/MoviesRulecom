John Goodman opened up on the “Happy Sad Confused” podcast about the disastrous launch of his latest film, “Digger.” He said the wave of negative headlines and pre‑release criticism “killed” the movie before it even opened its doors. The $160 million production, co‑starring Tom Cruise and directed by Alejandro G. Iñárritu, earned just $15 million domestically and has struggled to reach $50 million worldwide after a month. Goodman’s candid remarks highlight how early buzz can shape a blockbuster’s financial fate.

<Callout type="takeaway">Negative pre‑release buzz can sink even a $160 million blockbuster.</Callout>

## What happened
John Goodman told listeners that headlines predicting a $5 million opening and scathing pre‑release commentary “killed us before we even opened,” effectively turning audiences away. The Variety report notes the film’s budget topped $160 million, yet its domestic debut pulled in only $15 million, leaving worldwide earnings under $50 million after a month in theaters.

The sci‑fi thriller pairs Goodman with Tom Cruise, who plays an oil tycoon facing an Antarctic disaster that threatens Europe. Directed by Alejandro G. Iñárritu, the movie was marketed as a high‑concept, big‑budget event, but the early critical narrative eclipsed its promotional push.

## Why it matters
The “Digger” debacle underscores how powerful pre‑release narratives have become in the digital age. Studios pour billions into production and marketing, yet a handful of negative soundbites can derail box‑office expectations, influencing theater owners, advertisers, and streaming deals. For audiences, it raises the question of whether early criticism should dictate viewing choices or if the film deserves a fair chance after release.

## How to think about it
When evaluating a new release, consider the source of early opinions and balance them against the film’s premise, cast, and your own genre preferences. For streaming watchers, give the movie a trial period after its theatrical run; audience scores often diverge from critic headlines. Studios can mitigate risk by staggering marketing pushes and allowing word‑of‑mouth to build post‑opening rather than front‑loading hype.
