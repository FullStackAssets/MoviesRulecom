At the Hollywood premiere of “The Further Mis‑Adventures of Cliff Booth,” Brad Pitt revealed that production on the long‑awaited “Ocean’s 14” has been shifted to “sometime late next year.” The fifth entry in the heist franchise was originally slated to begin filming next month after a delay from its June start date. The postponement extends the gap since the 2023 tease of a “great” script and keeps the ensemble cast—Clooney, Roberts, Damon, and Cheadle—in limbo. Fans and studios are watching the timeline because the franchise’s box‑office momentum and streaming rights depend on a timely release. The update also signals how the industry’s crowded slate is affecting even high‑profile sequels.

<Callout type="takeaway">Ocean’s 14 won’t start filming until late 2027, delaying the franchise’s next installment.</Callout>

## What happened
Brad Pitt told reporters at the premiere that the shoot will begin “sometime late next year,” pushing the start date to late 2027. The film had been postponed from an initial June schedule and was expected to start next month before the latest shift.

The project retains its original ensemble—George Clooney, Julia Roberts, Matt Damon, Don Cheadle and Pitt himself—with David Leitch attached to direct. Pitt added that writers Grant and George have delivered a solid script, echoing Clooney’s 2023 comment about a “great” screenplay.

## Why it matters
Delaying production stalls the franchise’s revenue pipeline, affecting box‑office projections and future streaming licensing deals. The extended timeline also gives the studio room to fine‑tune the script, which could improve critical reception, but it risks losing audience momentum built by the earlier installments.
