The film world mourned the loss of Sebastián Lelio, the Chilean writer‑director whose Oscar‑winning drama A Fantastic Woman brought global attention to Chilean cinema. Lelio was found dead at his home in Santiago’s Las Condes district on Friday, according to El Mercurio Online. He was 52 and survived by his partner Amalia Kassai. His death marks the end of a career that reshaped international perceptions of Latin American storytelling. Fans and industry insiders alike are reflecting on his influential body of work.

## What happened
Sebastián Lelio, the Oscar‑winning director of A Fantastic Woman, was discovered deceased by his partner on Friday in his Santiago home, with Chilean news outlet El Mercurio Online confirming his age as 52. The announcement sparked an outpouring of tributes from festivals, actors, and critics who highlighted his contributions to contemporary cinema.

Lelio’s filmography includes the Berlin‑premiered Gloria (2013), its American remake Gloria Bell (2018), the psychological thriller Disobedience (2017), the religious‑themed The Wonder (2022), and his most recent Cannes entry The Wave (2025). He was also developing a new adaptation titled Chilean Poet at the time of his death.

## Why it matters
Lelio’s work broke barriers for Chilean and LGBTQ narratives on the world stage, proving that stories from the region could achieve both critical acclaim and commercial success. His Oscar win for A Fantastic Woman was the first for a Chilean film in the foreign‑language category, inspiring a generation of Latin American filmmakers to pursue bold, socially relevant projects. The industry now faces the loss of a visionary who consistently championed under‑represented voices.
