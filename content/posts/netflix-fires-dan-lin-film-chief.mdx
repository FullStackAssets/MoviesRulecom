Netflix announced the departure of Dan Lin, the head of its film division, after nearly three years in the role. The move comes on the heels of a massive layoff that trimmed roughly 1,000 jobs across the company, including key members of Lin’s team. Lin’s exit signals a deeper restructuring of Netflix’s movie strategy as the streamer battles stagnant subscriber growth and a costly failed bid for Warner Bros. Executives expect a new film chief to be named in the coming days, but the timing remains uncertain.
<Callout type="takeaway">Netflix’s removal of its film chief underscores a strategic pivot amid cost cuts and leadership turmoil.</Callout>
## What happened
Variety reports that Dan Lin was dismissed as Netflix’s film chief early Saturday, with the decision confirmed by two unnamed sources. The announcement follows a week‑long layoff that saw about 1,000 employees let go, including Lin’s right‑hand Doug Belgrad and indie head Sheroum Kim.

Lin, who joined Netflix in 2024 after leading the Rideback production company, had greenlit or acquired 88 titles during his tenure, including the animated “Lego” franchise and Stephen King adaptations. A Netflix spokesperson declined to comment, and no replacement has been identified, though Warner Bros. veterans Mike De Luca and Pam Abdy are rumored to be in talks.
## Why it matters
The film division has been a cornerstone of Netflix’s content budget, and Lin’s departure raises questions about the platform’s ability to secure high‑profile movies. With subscriber growth slowing and the stock under pressure, the company may shift toward lower‑cost productions or lean more heavily on television and original series. Competitors such as Disney+ and Amazon Prime are also intensifying their film slates, so Netflix’s leadership gap could affect its market share.
