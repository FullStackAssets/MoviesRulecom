The 30th Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival just unveiled a haunting glimpse of Janno Jürgens’ new psychological drama. An exclusive clip of “No Me Without You” premiered, introducing estranged sisters Eva Koldits and Katariina Unt who are forced together by tragedy. Jürgens says the sister bond is \"deeply primal,\" mixing love and rivalry in a way that feels both intimate and unsettling. The film’s focus on sibling psychology could reshape how audiences view family thrillers.

<Callout type="takeaway">Jürgens uses the sister relationship to probe the thin line between love and hate.</Callout>

## What happened
At the 30th Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival an exclusive clip of “No Me Without You” was shown for the first time. The film, written by Jürgens and Anti Naulainen, follows two estranged sisters—Eva Koldits and Katariina Unt—who are reunited by a tragic event that forces them to confront shared guilt and moral ambiguity.

Jürgens explained his fascination with the sibling dynamic, noting, \"There’s something deeply primal about it. A sister or brother can be the person closest to you, but also your very first rival.\" He also emphasized involving the actresses in detailed screenplay discussions to capture an authentic women’s perspective.

The project is sold by Loco Films and produced by Marju Lepp for Filmivabrik, positioning it as a dark psychological thriller that builds on Jürgens’ earlier feature, “Rain.”

## Why it matters
By centering on the love‑hate tension between sisters, the film taps into universal family dynamics while pushing the psychological‑thriller genre into more intimate territory. Its exploration of guilt, memory, and the possibility of reconciliation resonates beyond the festival circuit, offering viewers a reflective lens on their own relationships. If the film succeeds, it could inspire more stories that treat sibling bonds with the same narrative weight traditionally given to romantic or parental ties.
