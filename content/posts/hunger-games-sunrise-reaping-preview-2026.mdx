Opening paragraph: Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has opened with $21.25 million in previews, the second‑best franchise total behind 2013’s Catching Fire. The Thursday‑night advance sales, driven by early‑access PLF showings and a $5 million PLF show, signal a strong opening potential. With a 95% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, the film is already outperforming many of its predecessors. These numbers matter because they set the tone for the Thanksgiving‑week box office and could shift the competitive landscape for mid‑season releases. Fans and investors alike are watching closely to see if the franchise can maintain its momentum.

## What happened

The preview window began on Wednesday with a $5 million PLF showing, followed by a $16.25 million haul on Thursday night, bringing the total to $21.25 million. This figure places Sunrise on the Reaping as the second‑best preview total for a Hunger Games title, only behind Catching Fire’s $25.2 million. It also tops the year‑to‑date preview leaderboard, trailing only Sony’s Spider‑Man: Brand New Day ($72 million) and ahead of Universal’s The Odyssey ($17.6 million).

In comparison, Universal’s Wicked posted $19.2 million in previews, while Lionsgate’s Michael earned $12.6 million. The Hunger Games: Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which faced a strike‑impacted campaign, managed only $5.75 million. Internationally, the film is projected to open between $70 million and $86 million overseas, boosting its worldwide prospects.

Critics have been lukewarm, with a 68% fresh rating on Rotten Tomatoes, but audience enthusiasm remains high, reflected in the 95% audience score. The director, Francis Lawrence, is returning for the fifth installment, following his work on the 2012 original.

## Why it matters

A strong preview performance can translate into a robust opening weekend, influencing distribution decisions and future franchise investments. The Hunger Games series has historically been a reliable source of revenue for Lionsgate, and a $100 million opening would reaffirm its viability amid a crowded market. Additionally, the film’s success could affect streaming rights negotiations and the timing of the next installment’s release. For fans, it signals that the narrative arc is still resonating, while for studios it underscores the importance of early‑access strategies.

## How to think about it

When evaluating a preview haul, consider the historical conversion rate from previews to opening weekend grosses for the franchise. Compare the percentage of preview sales relative to the film’s total budget and previous installments. Also factor in the competitive landscape—if other major releases are slated for the same week, the film may need to rely more on marketing momentum than on preview strength alone. Finally, monitor audience sentiment on social platforms; a high audience score can be a more reliable predictor than critic reviews.
