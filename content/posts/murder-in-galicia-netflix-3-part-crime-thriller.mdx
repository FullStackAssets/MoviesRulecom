Netflix’s newest addition to its crime lineup has quickly climbed the global charts, proving that high-stakes true crime adaptations still dominate the streaming landscape. Murder in Galicia, a three-episode limited series, debuted on November 13 and has already secured the number three spot on the platform’s worldwide viewership list. The series has surged past other major titles like Black Doves season 2 and Lupin season 4, signaling a significant shift in international audience engagement. Despite this global momentum, the show has yet to crack the Top 10 in the United States, making it a standout hidden gem for viewers looking for a fresh, high-quality binge.

<Callout type="takeaway"> Murder in Galicia is a fast-paced, two-hour binge that effectively dramatizes a real 2015 Spanish murder case, offering a sharp contrast to the documentary coverage of the same events.</Callout>

## What happened
The series, also known as El crimen de Pazos, adapts the real-life 2015 murder of Isabel Fuentes in rural Galicia, Spain. The narrative follows police investigator Elías Lago as he probes a brutal attack on a woman named Irene Freire in the village of Pazos. While the initial evidence suggests a robbery, Lago suspects that Irene’s husband, Aniceto, is the perpetrator despite their outwardly happy marriage.

The show consists of three episodes, each running approximately 43 to 44 minutes, for a total runtime of two hours and ten minutes. This compact format allows the story to maintain a lightning-fast pace, moving quickly through the investigation as Irene remains hospitalized and new clues point to her continued danger. The series serves as a fictionalized companion to Unprotected: Murder in Galicia, a true crime documentary released earlier this year that chronicles the same real-life events.

## Why it matters
The rapid rise of Murder in Galicia highlights the enduring appeal of true crime adaptations, particularly when they are packaged in short, bingeable formats. By placing the show in the top three globally within its first five days, Netflix demonstrates the strength of its international content strategy. The series appeals to a broad audience of crime drama fans who prefer tight, plot-driven narratives over longer, slower-burning procedurals. Its success also underscores the potential for Spanish-language or European productions to achieve mainstream global traction without heavy marketing pushes in the U.S. market.

If you are looking for a crime thriller that respects your time, this series is a strong candidate for your weekend viewing.
