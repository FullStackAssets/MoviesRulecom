The BBC has unveiled a new cost‑saving plan that will see more than 100 newsroom roles disappear and two of its flagship TV channels disappear from the airwaves. Director of BBC News Rachel Jupp announced the proposal in an email to staff, outlining £5.5 million in immediate savings. The plan also includes shutting down the linear broadcast of BBC Three and BBC Scotland in spring 2027, moving those services to iPlayer and online only. Radio 4 Extra is slated for full closure, while the corporation continues its broader £500 million reduction target by 2028. The moves signal a major shift in how the public broadcaster delivers news and entertainment.

<Callout type="takeaway">BBC News’ cost‑saving plan will trim over 100 jobs and move key TV channels online, reshaping how British audiences receive news.</Callout>

## What happened
On Tuesday, BBC News director Rachel Jupp shared a proposal that would eliminate just over 100 positions, saving roughly £5.5 million. The cuts are part of a £500 million spending reduction programme the BBC aims to meet by 2028.

In addition, the corporation said BBC Three and BBC Scotland will cease linear broadcasting in spring 2027, with content migrating to the iPlayer platform. Radio 4 Extra will be closed entirely, and the news division is expected to contribute at least £51 million toward the overall savings goal, having already identified about £30.5 million in savings.

The proposal follows Jupp’s appointment as news director and builds on earlier plans that targeted 550 jobs by the 2027‑28 financial year.

## Why it matters
The cuts strike at the heart of the BBC’s news operation, reducing staff at a time when digital competition is intensifying. Removing linear channels like BBC Three reduces traditional TV reach, pushing audiences toward streaming, which could affect advertising and licence fee perceptions. The financial pressure also forces the corporation to rethink how it allocates resources across television, radio, and digital journalism, potentially reshaping editorial priorities.
