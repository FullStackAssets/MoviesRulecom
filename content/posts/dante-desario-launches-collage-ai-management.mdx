Former UTA film agent Dante DeSario has taken a bold step into the AI frontier. He announced the launch of Collage, a Los Angeles‑based management firm dedicated to AI‑native production companies. The firm closed a seed round backed by Black Bear, Fundamental Films and a roster of veteran industry investors. By bridging AI studios with Hollywood studios, streamers and brands, Collage could reshape how content is created and financed.

<Callout type="takeaway">Collage aims to be the conduit that turns AI‑native creativity into mainstream Hollywood deals.</Callout>

## What happened
Collage launched with seed funding from Black Bear and Fundamental Films, joined by investors and advisors such as Dennis Miller, GOAL Ventures, AGC Studios, Roy Lee and others. Its initial client roster includes AI‑focused studios like Asteria, Defect, Lipstick, Matriarch, Native Foreign, Obsidian, Secret Level and Lux Meridian, many of which have already delivered festival selections and high‑profile VFX work.

DeSario brings years of experience from UTA’s independent film group, where he packaged and financed titles such as Jordan Firstman’s Club Kid, which sold to A24 for a high‑teens‑million deal, as well as projects like Hokum (Neon) and Tenzing (Apple). His track record of bridging talent and capital positions him to guide AI‑native companies through Hollywood’s complex deal landscape.

## Why it matters
The AI boom has moved from speculative hype to a pragmatic phase where studios are actively scouting AI‑driven content. Collage’s role as a specialized manager could accelerate the integration of AI tools into mainstream production, offering faster, cheaper visual effects while also raising questions about creative authorship and job security for traditional crew members.

## How to think about it
For creators, consider partnering with a Collage‑backed studio to gain access to studio financing while retaining AI‑centric creative control. For studios and streamers, use Collage as a scouting hub to evaluate AI‑native projects before committing large budgets. Evaluate each AI‑driven pitch on storytelling merit, not just technological novelty.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume every AI‑native project will be a hit; the market is still volatile and audience acceptance varies.</Callout>
