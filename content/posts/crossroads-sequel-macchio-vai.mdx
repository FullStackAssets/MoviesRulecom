The 1986 guitar‑driven cult classic Crossroads is poised for a comeback. Screenwriter John Fusco revealed in a Guitar World interview that he and star Ralph Macchio have a concrete plan for a sequel. The project aims to shift focus from Eugene Martone to a contemporary guitarist while bringing back Steve Vai’s metal‑blues antagonist Jack Butler. If realized, the film could tap into the recent wave of ’80s sequels that have found eager audiences. Fans of the original are already buzzing about the potential return of its iconic music battles.
<Callout type="takeaway">A Crossroads sequel is officially in development, with Macchio and Vai slated to reprise their roles.</Callout>
## What happened
John Fusco told Guitar World that he and Macchio have been discussing a follow‑up for years and now have a plan. He said the idea emerged from an “anniversary thing” conversation and that the sequel would be “exciting to music fans, to blues purists.”

Macchio confirmed the concept will likely feature a new protagonist relevant to today’s music scene, moving the story away from his character Eugene Martone. He emphasized he hasn’t given up on the project despite its secrecy.

Steve Vai expressed enthusiasm about returning as Jack Butler, joking that Macchio wants to “kick my ass” and that Butler “needs his revenge.” Vai may need to dust off his iconic red Jackson guitar for the role.
## Why it matters
A sequel could re‑energize the guitar‑movie genre and introduce the Crossroads legacy to a new generation, while giving longtime fans a chance to revisit the film’s legendary duels. It also signals Hollywood’s broader trend of mining ’80s cult classics for fresh content, which can boost streaming libraries and theatrical interest. However, the project’s success will hinge on balancing nostalgia with a story that feels relevant to today’s music culture.
