The film world mourns the loss of Sebastián Lelio, the Chilean director whose Oscar‑winning drama ‘A Fantastic Woman’ reshaped global conversations about trans representation. Lelio died at his home in Las Condes, Santiago, at the age of 52, as announced by his partner Amalia Kassai on Instagram. His death marks the end of a career that blended intimate storytelling with bold social commentary, influencing both Latin American cinema and the broader international market. Fans and industry professionals alike are reflecting on his legacy and the void his absence creates.

## What happened
Chilean filmmaker Sebastián Lelio was reported dead on November 6, 2026, after passing away at his Las Condes residence. The announcement came from his partner Amalia Kassai via Instagram, which confirmed the loss but did not disclose a cause.
Lelio, born in Argentina and raised in Chile, rose to prominence with the 2013 drama Gloria, which earned lead actress Paulina García a Berlin award. He achieved international acclaim with the 2017 Oscar‑winning ‘A Fantastic Woman’, the first Chilean film to win Best International Feature. Subsequent English‑language projects such as ‘Gloria Bell’, ‘The Wonder’, and his final 2025 film ‘T’ expanded his reputation across festivals and streaming platforms.
