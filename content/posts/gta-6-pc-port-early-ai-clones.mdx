Rockstar North’s former technical director Obbe Vermeij says the upcoming GTA 6 PC version could arrive well before the typical console‑first window. He warns that generative‑AI tools are already being used to recreate the PlayStation 5 build, threatening Rockstar’s revenue. An accelerated PC launch would give Take‑Two a chance to protect its intellectual property and capture sales from the large PC audience. The comment comes as the PS5 edition is slated for a 19 November release, putting pressure on the studio’s schedule. If true, the move could reshape how major publishers time PC ports.

<Callout type="takeaway">An early GTA 6 PC release is being considered to stay ahead of AI‑generated copies.</Callout>

## What happened

Vermeij told Rock Paper Shotgun that Rockstar and its publisher Take‑Two are concerned about unofficial generative‑AI clones of the PS5 version once it launches on 19 November. He described the threat of “chat‑bot herders” duplicating the game as "the big question" for developers today.

He also recounted Rockstar’s early history, noting that the original GTA titles were built for PC before being handed to an external team for PlayStation, often resulting in texture downgrades. After GTA 3 the studio flipped to a console‑first strategy, but the PC market has since caught up, leaving a lingering mindset that could influence the timing of the GTA 6 PC port.

## Why it matters

Releasing the PC version early would let Rockstar monetize a platform that traditionally follows consoles, reducing the window for AI‑generated copies to circulate and eroding sales. It also signals to other publishers that protecting IP from generative‑AI threats may require schedule adjustments, potentially reshaping release strategies across the industry. For gamers, an earlier PC launch could mean access to the game sooner, but it also raises concerns about the polish and performance of a rushed port.
