Fans of the Fanning sisters finally get to see them share the screen in the upcoming WWII drama ‘The Nightingale’. The film, adapted from Kristin Hannah’s bestseller, follows French sisters Vianne and Isabelle as they navigate Nazi‑occupied France. Vanity Fair’s exclusive first‑look reveals Dakota and Elle’s on‑set chemistry and their reflections on working together for the first time. The movie is slated for a March 19, 2027 theatrical release, positioning the sisters as a new on‑screen duo.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Fanning sisters’ first joint performance adds authentic sibling chemistry to a high‑stakes WWII drama.</Callout>

## What happened
Vanity Fair published the first‑look images this week, showing Dakota and Elle Fanning rehearsing a tense resistance scene. In the interview, Dakota described the experience as “nerve‑wracking” but said the sisters fell back on a lifetime of playing together at home, making the process feel natural.

The Nightingale adapts Kristin Hannah’s novel about two French sisters—older Vianne, who shelters Jewish children, and younger Isabelle, who joins the Resistance. Production wrapped in New York earlier this year, and the film is set to open in theaters on March 19, 2027 before moving to streaming.

## Why it matters
Seeing real siblings portray close sisters adds a layer of authenticity that can deepen audience engagement, especially in a story centered on familial sacrifice and resistance. The pairing also creates a fresh marketing hook for both theatrical and streaming platforms, potentially drawing viewers who followed the Fannings’ separate careers. Moreover, the film contributes to the growing slate of WWII dramas that highlight women’s roles in the conflict, expanding representation beyond traditional male‑focused narratives.
