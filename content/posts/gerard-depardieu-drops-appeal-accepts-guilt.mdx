Gerard Depardieu, the iconic French actor, announced Monday that he is dropping his appeal against a sexual assault conviction. The court had sentenced him to an 18‑month suspended term and placed him on France’s national sex‑offender registry. In a statement titled “I hear,” he accepted responsibility and said he now understands the line between humor and offense. The decision comes as the first successful #MeToo‑style case against the veteran star, signaling a shift in how French courts handle industry misconduct. Observers say the move could influence ongoing investigations into other alleged incidents.

<Callout type="takeaway">Depardieu’s admission marks a rare legal acknowledgment of sexual misconduct in French cinema.</Callout>

## What happened
On Monday, Depardieu filed a formal notice withdrawing his appeal of the conviction handed down last year for sexually assaulting two women—a production designer and an assistant director—on the set of Jean Becker’s The Green Shutters in 2021. The court’s original ruling imposed an 18‑month suspended sentence and required him to register on the national sex‑offender list.

In the statement sent to French media, Depardieu said, “I have been found guilty. I hear it. I accept this and take full responsibility for the conviction,” and reflected on his past behavior on film sets. His lawyer, Delphine Meillet, described the decision as the result of “a long period of reflection and personal growth.”

## Why it matters
The case is a landmark for the #MeToo movement in France, showing that even the most powerful figures in the entertainment industry can be held criminally accountable. It may embolden other victims to come forward and push studios to adopt stricter on‑set conduct policies. At the same time, the suspended nature of the sentence and the fact that many other allegations remain unresolved highlight the limits of legal remedies in addressing systemic abuse.
