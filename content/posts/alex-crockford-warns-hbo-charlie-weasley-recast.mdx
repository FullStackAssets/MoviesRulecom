Alex Crockford, the actor who played Charlie Weasley in the original Harry Potter films, took to X to congratulate Harry Gilby, the newcomer cast as the same character in HBO’s upcoming series. Crockford’s tongue‑in‑cheek post highlighted that his own screen time as Charlie amounted to just 1.5 seconds. The tweet has sparked conversation among fans about how much attention a minor character will receive in the new adaptation. As the series promises a more faithful retelling of Rowling’s books, the joke underscores the heightened scrutiny on every casting choice.

<Callout type="takeaway">Even a 1.5‑second cameo can become a talking point when a beloved franchise is rebooted.</Callout>

## What happened
The HBO adaptation of Harry Potter announced Harry Gilby as the new Charlie Weasley, adding him to a cast that aims to cover the entire wizarding world. Shortly after the news broke, Alex Crockford posted on X, congratulating Gilby and quipping, “I hope you get more screen time than I did (the record to beat is 1.5 seconds!).”

Crockford’s comment referenced his own brief appearance: Charlie Weasley never spoke on screen in the original films and was only visible for a split second in a background photograph during *Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban* (2004). In the books, Charlie is a dragonologist who appears in *Goblet of Fire* and later helps the resistance in *Deathly Hallows*, but the movies never gave him a substantive role.

## Why it matters
The exchange highlights how even the smallest roles can generate fan interest when a franchise is rebooted, especially on social media where nostalgia fuels discussion. It also signals that HBO is paying attention to fan‑favorite characters, even those with limited screen time, potentially expanding their presence in the series. For viewers, the joke sets expectations that the new adaptation may give minor characters more depth, influencing how audiences anticipate the show’s fidelity to the books.
