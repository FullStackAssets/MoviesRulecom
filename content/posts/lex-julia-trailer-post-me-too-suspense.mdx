The latest trailer for Lex Julia drops today, pulling back the curtain on a tense, post‑MeToo drama set on a remote Baltic island. Directed by Finnish auteur Laura Hyppönen, the film follows music producer Julia as she confronts a man from her traumatic past during a weekend visit. The clip, unveiled ahead of the film’s world premiere at the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival on Nov 13, showcases the atmospheric tension and moral ambiguity at the heart of the story. Its arrival signals a bold addition to the growing slate of European cinema tackling sexual violence with suspenseful storytelling.
<Callout type="takeaway">Lex Julia uses island isolation to magnify the complexities of consent and memory in a post‑MeToo thriller.</Callout>
## What happened
The trailer was released on October 20 via The Hollywood Reporter, confirming that Lex Julia will world‑premiere in the Baltic Film Competition at the 30th Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival on Nov 13. The Finnish‑Polish‑Estonian‑Swedish co‑production stars Rea Lest, Christian Hillborg and Jessica Grabowsky, and is slated for a theatrical launch in Finland in January through StoryHill.

Set on an isolated island villa, the narrative follows Julia, a music producer in her early thirties, who visits her friend Anna only to recognize Anna’s husband JP as the man linked to a past sexual assault. The story pivots on whether the encounter was a one‑night stand or rape, and how each character negotiates shame, responsibility and truth.

Hyppönen describes the film as a character‑driven suspense piece that bends the conventions of the rape‑revenge genre, keeping the threat of violence present while exploring unexpected ways the characters cope with tension.
## Why it matters
Lex Julia arrives at a moment when European cinema is increasingly confronting the aftermath of the MeToo movement, offering a suspenseful lens rather than a straightforward courtroom drama. By situating the trauma on an isolated island, the film forces viewers to confront how memory and power dynamics play out in confined spaces, prompting discussions about consent, victim agency, and societal shame. Its festival platform ensures the conversation reaches an international audience, potentially influencing how future filmmakers handle similar subject matter.
