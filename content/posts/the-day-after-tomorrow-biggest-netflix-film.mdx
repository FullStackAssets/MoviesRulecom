When Netflix released its latest streaming rankings, the 2004 disaster thriller The Day After Tomorrow surged to the top spot in the United States. The 123‑minute film, starring Jake Gyllenhaal as Sam Hall, now sits at No. 1, overtaking newer releases. Its resurgence is fueled by renewed interest in climate‑disaster narratives and the star’s growing fan base. The movie’s original box‑office haul of more than $552 million shows it still resonates with audiences. This shift highlights how older titles can dominate streaming charts and reshape a performer’s legacy.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Day After Tomorrow’s streaming triumph repositions Jake Gyllenhaal’s early work as a modern streaming staple.</Callout>

## What happened
The Day After Tomorrow premiered in May 2004 under director Roland Emmerich. Though critics were mixed, the film earned over $552 million worldwide against a $125 million budget, becoming Emmerich’s second‑highest‑grossing title after Independence Day.

In November 2026 Netflix’s internal charts listed the 123‑minute disaster thriller as the No. 1 movie in the U.S., surpassing recent hits like Ben Affleck’s Animals. The spike follows a promotional push and a wave of climate‑themed viewing.

## Why it matters
The resurgence demonstrates the power of streaming platforms to revive legacy films, giving them new cultural relevance and revenue streams long after theatrical runs. For Jake Gyllenhaal, the renewed visibility reinforces his versatility and introduces his early work to a younger audience, potentially influencing casting decisions and fan perception. It also signals that audiences are hungry for high‑stakes, climate‑driven narratives, prompting studios to reconsider the longevity of disaster genre libraries.
