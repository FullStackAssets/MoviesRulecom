The iconic slasher franchise Friday the 13th is gearing up for its first theatrical outing in almost two decades. Veteran horror director Steve Miner, who gave Jason his hockey mask in Part III, has been tapped to helm the 2027 film. The project, scripted by emerging writer David Fliegel, is being financed by the Barsamian family’s Horror, Inc. and will be produced in partnership with CatchLight Studios. With Cineverse handling North American distribution, the movie arrives on the heels of the Peacock/A24 series Crystal Lake, promising to bridge the franchise’s past and a new generation of fans. Its independent‑backed model signals a shift away from major studio control.

<Callout type="takeaway">Steve Miner’s return marks the franchise’s first theatrical revival in nearly 20 years.</Callout>

## What happened
The announcement confirmed that Steve Miner will direct the next Friday the 13th entry, marking his first return to the series since he introduced the hockey‑mask in Part III. The film, slated for a 2027 release, comes from a script by David Fliegel and will be produced under the independent banner of Horror, Inc., the company owned by the Barsamian family.

Financing and production are being handled by Horror, Inc. with CatchLight Studios as a production partner, while Cineverse has secured North American theatrical rights. This marks the first theatrical Friday the 13th film since the 2009 Warner Bros. reboot and follows the recent Peacock/A24 series Crystal Lake, positioning the new movie as a bridge between legacy fans and newcomers.

## Why it matters
Re‑uniting the franchise with its early‑era director offers a sense of continuity that longtime fans cherish, while the independent financing promises greater creative control over tone and practical effects. The involvement of Cineverse for distribution suggests a theatrical push that could revitalize the slasher genre in a market dominated by streaming releases. For the broader horror community, the project signals renewed confidence in legacy franchises as viable box‑office draws.
