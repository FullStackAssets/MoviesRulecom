Former President Donald Trump sat down with 60 Minutes correspondent Ross Douthat for his first interview since the midterm elections. He reflected on the Republican Party’s sweeping losses and responded to Vice President‑designate JD Vance’s four‑point post‑mortem. Trump argued that his campaign efforts and the party’s stance on the war, inflation, and Israel shaped the outcome. The conversation offers a rare glimpse into how the GOP’s leadership is framing its defeat ahead of the 2028 cycle. For streaming audiences, the interview underscores the political weight of high‑profile media events.

<Callout type="takeaway">Trump uses the interview to align with Vance’s critique while emphasizing a hard line on Iran.</Callout>

## What happened
In the interview, Trump said he was not on the ballot but campaigned hard, noting massive rallies and a base that loved him. He suggested that voters who turned out for him were forced to support Republican candidates they disliked, and he blamed dissatisfaction with the war, rising gasoline prices, and a “great dislike” for Israel for the losses.

A few days earlier, JD Vance had posted a detailed analysis outlining four voter priorities: ending the war, lowering the cost of living and inflation, putting America first, and reshaping the U.S.–Israel relationship. Douthat asked Trump if he agreed, and Trump replied, “I don’t disagree with it… you write what you want.” He added that he wished Vance had highlighted the need to prevent Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon, calling that a “big thing.”

Douthat also pointed to polling that shows Americans unhappy with the economy, but Trump countered that the economy remained strong during his second term, framing cost‑of‑living concerns as political narratives rather than economic realities.

## Why it matters
The interview signals how the GOP’s top figures are publicly framing the midterm defeat, which could shape messaging for upcoming primaries and the 2028 presidential race. By echoing Vance’s four points, Trump reinforces a narrative that blames foreign policy and cost‑of‑living issues, potentially steering future campaign platforms toward hawkish stances on Iran and a recalibrated approach to Israel. For streaming viewers, the high‑profile sit‑down illustrates how traditional broadcast interviews continue to drive political discourse in a digital‑first media environment.
