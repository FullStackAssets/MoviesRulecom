When Pixar’s in‑house lab drops a new experimental short, it signals fresh creative freedom for the studio’s artists. The latest release, "Love on a Plate," is a six‑minute animated piece that celebrates food, family, and community. Directed by veteran Pixar artist Ana G. Lacaze and produced by Jessica Heidt, the film draws on the phenakistoscope, an early animation device, to shape its visual language. Premiered on Pixar’s official YouTube channel, the short gives audiences a glimpse into the personal stories that inspire the studio’s talent.

<Callout type="takeaway">‘Love on a Plate’ shows how Pixar’s artist‑driven shorts can turn intimate family memories into universal stories.</Callout>

## What happened
Pixar unveiled "Love on a Plate" on November 19, 2026 via its YouTube channel as part of the Pixar Artist Shorts series. The short runs just a few minutes, presenting a whimsical tale of a grandmother’s cooking, the love it nurtures, and the way food brings generations together.

The project was directed by Ana G. Lacaze, a longtime Pixar visual effects and lighting artist who has contributed to films from *Monsters, Inc.* to *Inside Out 2*. Produced by Jessica Heidt, the film was created under Pixar Lab, an internal experimental program that encourages artists to explore personal concepts outside the main feature pipeline. Its visual style references the phenakistoscope, an early spinning disc animation technique, giving the short a hand‑drawn, cyclical feel.

## Why it matters
By releasing a personal, artist‑driven short, Pixar signals that its creative talent is being given space to experiment beyond blockbuster features. The focus on food and community taps into universal themes that resonate across cultures, reinforcing Pixar’s reputation for storytelling that blends emotion with everyday life. For fans, the short offers a fresh entry point to see how individual artists translate their own histories into the studio’s signature animation quality.
