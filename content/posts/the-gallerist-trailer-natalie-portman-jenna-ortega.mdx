The Gallerist, a dark comedy set against the glitz of Art Basel Miami, just dropped its first trailer. The teaser spotlights Natalie Portman and Jenna Ortega as they navigate a macabre art‑world scheme that spirals out of control. With a Dec. 4 theatrical debut, the trailer’s buzz signals a fresh satirical take on influencer culture and the high‑stakes gallery scene.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Gallerist blends satire and suspense, positioning it as a standout dark comedy for the holiday season.</Callout>

## What happened
The first trailer for The Gallerist was released by Roadside Attractions and Vertical after the film’s Sundance premiere earlier this year. It showcases Portman as an ambitious gallery owner who, in a desperate move, passes off an accidental corpse as the centerpiece of her debut show, setting off a chaotic chain of deception as influencers and police converge.

The dark comedy, co‑written by director Cathy Yan and James Pedersen, unfolds at Art Basel Miami and promises a ticking‑clock chaos that mixes art‑world satire with a twisted heist plot. The ensemble also features Da’Vine Joy Randolph, Sterling K. Brown, Zach Galifianakis, Daniel Brühl, Charli XCX and Catherine Zeta‑Jones.

The film is slated to open in theaters on December 4, positioning it as a late‑year counter‑program to the usual holiday fare.

## Why it matters
The Gallerist taps into growing audience appetite for genre‑bending stories that critique social media and elite culture. By framing a murder‑turned‑art piece within the high‑profile Art Basel setting, the film spotlights the absurd lengths to which the art market will go for buzz, resonating with both cinephiles and industry insiders. Its star‑studded cast and director Cathy Yan’s reputation for sharp, socially aware storytelling raise expectations for a commercially viable yet thought‑provoking release.

## How to think about it
Approach the film as a satire that uses exaggeration to comment on real‑world art market dynamics. Viewers should watch for how the narrative juxtaposes the glamour of Art Basel with the grim reality of the protagonist’s desperate choices, and consider the broader critique of influencer culture that underpins the comedy.
