Clayface, the R‑rated DC horror thriller directed by James Watkins, has crossed the $109 million worldwide mark only two weeks into its theatrical run. The film earned $56.6 million domestically and $52.4 million overseas, surpassing its $40‑44 million production budget and reaching break‑even. At the same time, exhibitors have pulled the movie from more than 530 screens, shrinking its theater count from an opening 3,458 locations. The juxtaposition of strong earnings and rapid theater loss highlights a rare profit path for a modest superhero title.

<Callout type="takeaway">Clayface proves a low‑budget DC film can turn a profit even as theaters drop it.</Callout>

## What happened
Clayface amassed $56.6 million in the United States and $52.4 million abroad, bringing its global total to $109 million, well above the estimated $40‑44 million production cost. The movie opened in 3,458 theaters but entered its third week with a loss of over 530 venues, a steep contraction for any release.

Despite the drop, the film added roughly $7 million over a recent weekend, nudging its domestic tally toward $60 million. Its performance outpaces other modest DC entries such as James Watkins’ own Speak No Evil ($77 million worldwide) and is on track to eclipse The Woman in Black and this year’s Supergirl, both of which earned around $130‑125 million on larger budgets.

## Why it matters
Clayface shows that a lean budget combined with strong critical reception—78 % Certified Fresh on Rotten Tomatoes—can offset a shrinking exhibition footprint. The result encourages DC Studios to consider more boutique, genre‑bending projects rather than relying solely on high‑budget tentpoles. However, the rapid theater loss also signals that the film’s momentum may be front‑loaded, limiting its long‑term box‑office ceiling.

## How to think about it
When evaluating a film’s success, look beyond total gross and examine per‑screen averages, theater‑count trends, and budget relative to earnings. A strong opening can generate enough momentum to break even even if exhibitors quickly trim showings. Studios can leverage such data to greenlight more cost‑controlled genre titles that rely on critical buzz rather than massive screen allocations.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the current profit trajectory will continue; the steep theater drop indicates the film may soon experience a steep revenue decline.</Callout>
