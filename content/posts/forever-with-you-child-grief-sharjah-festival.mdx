Italian director Fabrizio Cattani debuted his new film “Forever With You” at the 13th Sharjah International Film Festival for Children and Youth. The drama follows a nine‑year‑old boy named Filo as he navigates the death of his mother through flashbacks and symbolic companions. By framing grief through a child’s innocent perspective, Cattani aims to prove that cinema must stay emotionally rooted. The premiere marks the film’s first Middle‑East showing and highlights Italy’s role as this year’s Country of Honor at the festival. For streaming fans, the movie signals a fresh, heart‑centered story that could soon move beyond the festival circuit.

<Callout type="takeaway">“Forever With You” shows that childhood lenses can turn mourning into a universally resonant adventure.</Callout>

## What happened
Italian filmmaker Fabrizio Cattani brought “Forever With You” to the Sharjah International Film Festival for Children and Youth, where it received a Green Carpet premiere. Adapted from an Italian novel, the film is set in 1998 and follows young Filo as he struggles to understand his mother’s death, using flashbacks to weave his inner journey.

Cattani explains that each supporting character symbolizes a facet of Filo’s psyche: his friend Rullo represents emotion, his father the mature side, Amelie the childish side, and Guelpho the frightening side. By externalizing these internal parts, the director creates a visual language that lets a child audience grapple with mourning without overt didacticism.

The festival, celebrating Italy as the Country of Honor, positioned the film as a bridge between European art cinema and Middle‑Eastern youth programming, underscoring the global relevance of emotionally grounded storytelling.

## Why it matters
Grief is rarely addressed directly in films aimed at younger viewers, leaving a gap that “Forever With You” deliberately fills. By anchoring the narrative in childhood innocence, the film invites both kids and adults to reflect on loss in a safe, symbolic space. Its festival debut also signals to streaming platforms that there is appetite for nuanced, emotionally driven family content that transcends simple comedy or adventure tropes.

## How to think about it
When you sit down to watch, let the symbolic characters guide your interpretation of Filo’s inner world rather than searching for literal plot points. For curators, consider pairing the film with discussion guides or companion shorts that explore grief, creating a mini‑program that adds educational value to your catalog.

<Callout type="warning">The film deals with the death of a parent and may be intense for very young children; parental guidance is recommended.</Callout>
