Peacock’s new “Friday the 13th” prequel, Crystal Lake, has quickly become a streaming milestone. In its first 11 days the series accumulated 1.3 billion minutes watched, equivalent to roughly 3.3 million full‑episode views. The performance lifts the show to the platform’s fourth‑most‑watched scripted original ever. With social buzz reaching 1.8 billion impressions in a single day, the buzz translates into tangible audience engagement. The rapid ascent signals both strong brand pull for legacy horror and a growing appetite for serialized thriller content on Peacock.

<Callout type="takeaway">Crystal Lake’s 1.3 billion minutes in 11 days makes it Peacock’s fourth‑best scripted debut.</Callout>

## What happened
According to Peacock, “Crystal Lake” logged 1.3 billion minutes in its first 11 days, which, divided by its 398‑minute runtime, translates to about 3.3 million complete views. The series now ranks as the fourth‑most‑watched scripted original on the service, behind “The Best Man: The Final Chapters,” “All Her Fault,” and “The Five Star Weekend” Season 1.

Nielsen data placed the show as the third‑most‑watched streaming original series and the third‑most‑streamed first‑season series across all television during the week of Oct. 19. Social‑media monitoring showed 1.8 billion impressions in a single day and more than 7.6 million video views over the 11‑day period.

## Why it matters
The numbers give Peacock a rare hit that can attract new subscribers and keep existing ones engaged, reinforcing the platform’s strategy to blend franchise power with original storytelling. Strong social engagement also offers advertisers a highly active audience, potentially boosting ad revenue. Finally, the success demonstrates that legacy horror properties can thrive in a serialized format, encouraging similar investments.

## How to think about it
When evaluating a streaming hit, weigh minutes‑watched against subscriber acquisition, retention, and ad revenue potential. Pair raw viewership data with social‑engagement signals to gauge audience enthusiasm. Use the performance as a benchmark, but diversify your slate to avoid over‑dependence on a single property.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that high minutes‑watched automatically equals a large unique audience; repeat watches can inflate the figure.</Callout>
