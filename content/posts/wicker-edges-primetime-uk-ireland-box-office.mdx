British‑Irish box office saw a surprise shift this weekend as Black Bear’s period comedy “Wicker” claimed the top spot, edging A24’s thriller “Primetime” by just over £10,000. The film earned £1.76 million, while “Primetime” posted £1.75 million. The narrow margin highlights the competitiveness of the market ahead of the releases of “Godzilla” and “Ramayana.” Industry observers note the result could influence distribution strategies for upcoming tentpole titles.

<Callout type="takeaway">A modest £10k edge gave “Wicker” the UK‑Ireland box‑office crown, underscoring how tight the market is before major releases.</Callout>

## What happened
"Wicker," directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson and starring Olivia Colman, Alexander Skarsgård, Peter Dinklage, and Elizabeth Debicki, opened at No. 1 with £1.76 million ($2.34 million). A24’s “Primetime,” starring Robert Pattinson and Merritt Wever, placed second with £1.75 million ($2.33 million), a razor‑thin gap of £10,141.

Lionsgate U.K.’s horror‑comedy “Ghosts: The Possession of Button House” fell to third with $2 million, while Warner Bros.’ “Clayface” held fourth with $1.6 million. Stop‑motion adventure “Wildwood,” Universal’s “Forgotten Island,” and Paramount’s “Street Fighter” rounded out the top ten, each adding modest sums to their cumulative totals.

## Why it matters
The result proves that modest‑budget period pieces can still out‑perform star‑driven thrillers in the UK‑Ireland market, especially when release windows avoid direct clash with franchise tentpoles. With “Godzilla” and the Indian epic “Ramayana” slated for the coming weeks, distributors will watch whether the current hierarchy holds or is quickly reshuffled by the larger‑scale releases. The tight margin also signals that marketing spend and opening‑day buzz can be decisive factors for mid‑budget titles.

Distributors should schedule mid‑budget releases in windows that avoid direct competition with major franchise openings, leverage targeted regional marketing, and monitor early‑week box‑office trends to adjust promotional spend.
