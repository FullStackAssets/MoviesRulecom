The latest installment, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, arrives as a prequel to the Katniss era, but critics say it feels like a cash grab that stretches the franchise thin. The film follows 16‑year‑old Haymitch Abernathy as he is drafted into the Games, only to defy President Snow and plot a sabotage. Despite a star‑studded cast, the movie is described as overwrought, boring, and lacking the urgency that made the original series resonate. Its reliance on retro‑style visuals and a convoluted plot leaves audiences questioning whether the franchise still has fresh stories to tell.

<Callout type="takeaway">The film fails to capture the original's urgency, turning a once‑thrilling saga into a tired, cash‑grab spectacle.</Callout>

## What happened

Sunrise on the Reaping premiered on Disney+ on November 10, 2026, as part of the Hunger Games franchise's fourth film. It is set before the first Games, focusing on 16‑year‑old Haymitch Abernathy, played by Joseph Zada, who is chosen during the annual reaping. The narrative follows his defiance of President Snow, portrayed by Ralph Fiennes, and his attempt to sabotage the Games with explosives.

Critics slammed the film for its overwrought tone, lackluster pacing, and unconvincing characters such as Drusilla Sickle (Glenn Close) and Caesar Flickerman (Kieran Culkin). Reviewers noted that the retro‑style television sets and the absence of a middle‑class audience watching the Games diminish the series' social commentary. The Guardian's review described it as a 'nadir of a prequel' and a 'cash grab for an exhausted franchise.'

## Why it matters

The film's reception signals a potential decline in the Hunger Games brand, affecting future projects and fan engagement. It highlights the challenge of expanding a beloved franchise without diluting its core themes. The negative response may influence Disney+'s strategy for sequels and spin‑offs.

## How to think about it

When evaluating franchise extensions, consider whether they add depth to the original narrative, maintain thematic integrity, and offer fresh stakes. Compare audience expectations to the film's execution, and assess whether the new material justifies the franchise's legacy.
