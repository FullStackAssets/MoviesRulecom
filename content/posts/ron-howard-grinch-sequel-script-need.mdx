Ron Howard has publicly stated that the highly anticipated ‘Grinch’ sequel will only proceed if the script meets his high standards. The decision follows the original 2000 film’s success and the cast’s iconic performances. Howard emphasized that neither he nor Jim Carrey will move forward without a 'great script', a sentiment echoed by the Hollywood Reporter. This stance underscores the weight of legacy and creative integrity in franchise filmmaking.

<Callout type="takeaway">The ‘Grinch’ sequel hinges on a perfect script before production can begin.</Callout>

## What happened
Ron Howard revealed the project is in development with Universal and Imagine Entertainment, but no script exists yet. He cited the original film’s status as an evergreen holiday classic and expressed a desire to match that quality. The writers attached—Alec Berg, Jeff Schaffer, and David Mandel—are working on the screenplay.

Howard also highlighted Jim Carrey’s discomfort during the first film’s production, noting that the contact lenses and makeup were claustrophobic and that Carrey once had to breathe into a paper bag between takes. He said he wants to make the process easier for Carrey this time.

The director stated he and Carrey will move cautiously, hoping the sequel will feel like a true continuation of the beloved story.

## Why it matters
The decision reflects how legacy and audience expectations shape modern franchise development. A subpar sequel could tarnish the original’s reputation and alienate fans. Conversely, a well-crafted follow‑up could reinforce the franchise’s cultural impact and generate new revenue. The project also illustrates the growing trend of studios waiting for a strong script before committing to sequels.
