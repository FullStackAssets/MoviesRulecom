The Doha Film Festival announced it will no longer screen the fantasy feature “Fing!” after a wave of sexual‑harassment allegations against its author and star David Walliams. Walliams, known for “Little Britain” and “Britain’s Got Talent,” is accused of grooming teenage fans as young as 15, inviting them to his home and dressing rooms, and pressuring young women for dates and sexual activity. The cancellation follows similar moves by the BBC and Sky, which have pulled Walliams‑related content amid the same allegations. The film, which debuted positively at Sundance and was set for an international launch in Doha, now faces an uncertain future. The decision highlights how personal conduct allegations can reverberate through festival line‑ups and distribution deals.

<Callout type="takeaway">Allegations against David Walliams forced the Doha Film Festival to drop “Fing!” and triggered broader content withdrawals.</Callout>

## What happened
The Doha Film Festival removed “Fing!” from its program after a joint investigation by the Guardian and ITV News alleged that Walliams invited teenage girls, some as young as 15, to stay over at his house and to his dressing rooms, and that he repeatedly pressured them for dates and sexual activity. The festival’s statement confirmed the screening was cancelled and ticket holders would be refunded.

The removal is part of a cascade of actions against Walliams‑related projects. The BBC announced it is pulling shows such as “Little Britain” from its streaming service, and Sky withdrew as the U.K. and Ireland broadcaster for “Fing!”. The fantasy feature, directed by Jeffrey Walker and starring Taika Waititi, Mia Wasikowska, and Iona Bell, had premiered at Sundance and was slated for its international launch in Doha.

## Why it matters
The incident underscores how allegations of personal misconduct can quickly affect a film’s exhibition, distribution, and the reputations of all parties involved. Festivals and broadcasters are increasingly pressured to act swiftly, even before legal conclusions, to protect brand integrity and audience trust. For creators, cast, and crew not implicated in the allegations, the fallout can mean lost exposure and revenue, raising questions about collective responsibility versus individual accountability.

## How to think about it
Festival programmers should develop clear policies that outline how to respond to allegations, including timelines for investigation, criteria for removal, and communication plans for ticket holders. Streaming platforms can adopt similar guidelines, offering transparency about why content is pulled and providing avenues for appeal. For viewers, staying informed about the context of a film’s removal helps balance concerns about supporting alleged perpetrators with the desire to see the work of uninvolved artists.
