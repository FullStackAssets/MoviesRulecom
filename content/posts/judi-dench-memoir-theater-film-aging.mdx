At 91, Dame Judi Dench sat down with CBS News to discuss the new memoir "Is It Too Late to Make a Run for It?" and her lifelong devotion to the stage. She joked about a Bananagrams game, noting that her macular degeneration makes the letters “almost blind” but her wit remains sharp. Dench traced her journey from a 1957 Ophelia at the Old Vic to an Oscar‑winning film career, yet she insists theater is where the magic truly lives. The interview offers a rare glimpse into how an iconic actress balances aging, criticism, and creative fulfillment.

## What happened
Judi Dench recalled playing Bananagrams with a friend, laughing that she “has all the vowels” even as macular degeneration clouds her vision. She emphasized that the game’s challenge mirrors her approach to acting: adapt, improvise, and keep the performance alive.

She began her career on stage in 1957 as Ophelia at London’s Old Vic, and her first starring film role arrived four decades later in “Mrs. Brown.” Despite early rejection from a producer who said “you have every single thing wrong with your face,” she persisted, later winning an Oscar for “Shakespeare in Love” and becoming the iconic M in eight James Bond films.

Dench’s new memoir, co‑written with longtime friend Brendan O’Hea, is structured as a series of candid conversations that blend personal anecdotes with reflections on the craft, offering readers an intimate portrait of an actress who refuses to let age define her art.

## Why it matters
Dench’s story highlights how veteran actors can reinvent themselves while confronting age‑related challenges, reinforcing the value of theater as a collaborative, ever‑evolving space. Her candidness about criticism and visual impairment provides a roadmap for younger performers navigating setbacks, and her memoir adds a rare primary source on the evolution of British theatre and cinema over seven decades.
