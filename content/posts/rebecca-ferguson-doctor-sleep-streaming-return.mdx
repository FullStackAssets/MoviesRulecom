Rebecca Ferguson’s 2019 Stephen King sequel *Doctor Sleep* has quietly re‑emerged on the streaming radar after a seven‑year gap. The supernatural thriller, which earned $72 million worldwide against a $45 million budget, finally cracked the VOD top‑10 in multiple territories this November. Its unexpected surge comes as Ferguson’s profile continues to rise through high‑profile projects on Prime Video and Apple TV+. The comeback highlights how older titles can find new life on digital platforms, reshaping their commercial legacy.

## What happened
The film debuted in 2019 as a sequel to *The Shining*, pairing Ferguson with Ewan McGregor under director Mike Flanagan. Despite a modest $45 million budget, it pulled $72 million globally, a respectable but not blockbuster performance, and left the streaming market without a permanent home.

Seven years later, the movie has surged back into the VOD top‑10 in several countries, driven by renewed interest in Stephen King adaptations and Ferguson’s growing star power. Collider reports the title is now officially staging a comeback, prompting platforms to negotiate licensing deals.

## Why it matters
The revival underscores the long tail value of genre films on streaming services, especially those tied to iconic literary franchises. For studios, a strong VOD showing can offset earlier theatrical shortfalls and open doors for future licensing revenue. For fans, the availability of *Doctor Sleep* expands access to a key piece of the Stephen King cinematic universe, potentially influencing viewership of upcoming adaptations.
