The Israeli‑made documentary “NAZA,” which examines the killings in Gaza, has just crossed the 1.5 million‑ticket mark worldwide. The milestone comes from screenings in 47 territories and places the film among the most‑watched documentaries of 2026. Its success signals strong audience appetite for urgent, politically charged nonfiction. At the same time, the film’s hybrid distribution strategy offers a template for future doc releases.
<Callout type="takeaway">“NAZA” proves that a hard‑hitting documentary can achieve blockbuster‑level theater attendance.</Callout>
## What happened
“NAZA” tallied more than 1.5 million theatrical admissions across 47 territories as of Nov. 6, ranking it among the year’s most‑watched documentaries. Co‑directed by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, the duo previously earned an Oscar for “No Other Land.” The film has generated $13.5 million in overseas box‑office receipts and about $1 million in the United States.
In Italy, the documentary opened on Sept. 29 and has drawn 717,517 admissions, making it the third‑highest‑grossing documentary in the country’s history behind “Fahrenheit 9/11” and “March of the Penguins.” In the U.S. and Canada, a self‑distributed launch on Sept. 30 followed by Neon’s wider rollout has produced 83,673 admissions. The U.K. and Ireland release on Oct. 15, handled by Dogwoof, has reached 102,509 tickets sold, including a live‑streamed Q&A that sold more than 7,000 seats in a single night.
## Why it matters
The film’s unprecedented reach turns a regional conflict into a global conversation, demonstrating that audiences will travel to theaters for hard‑hitting nonfiction. Its box‑office performance validates hybrid distribution models that combine self‑distribution with established partners, offering a roadmap for future documentary releases. By occupying mainstream screens, “NAZA” pressures other media outlets to address the Gaza narrative, potentially shaping public opinion and policy discourse.
