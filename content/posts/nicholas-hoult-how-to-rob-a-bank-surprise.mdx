Nicholas Hoult leads the new heist thriller “How to Rob a Bank,” and early reviews are already calling it one of 2026’s biggest surprises. Critics from That Hollywood Show praised its unexpected emotional depth and fresh take on the genre. Director David Leitch delivers kinetic chases and stylized editing, but some note uneven character writing. The buzz suggests the film could reshape expectations for mainstream action movies this year.

<Callout type="takeaway">The film’s bold blend of emotion and high‑octane action makes it a standout surprise of the year.</Callout>

## What happened
Critics highlighted that the movie is “shocking in its message,” offering more than typical dumb action and delivering a good dose of emotion that resonates with audiences frustrated by current societal issues. The reviewers noted that the film feels fresh, with David Leitch taking daring risks and presenting an original spin on the classic bank‑heist formula.

Another set of reactions praised the kinetic, high‑octane police chases, shootouts, and stylized editing that keep viewers engaged, while also pointing out that the character writing falls short, making it Leitch’s weakest effort despite its visual flair.

## Why it matters
The strong reaction signals a shift toward heist movies that try to marry spectacle with social commentary, challenging the notion that big‑budget action must sacrifice depth. If the film succeeds commercially, studios may green‑light more genre hybrids that prioritize both thrills and thematic relevance, affecting directors, writers, and audiences alike.

## How to think about it
Treat the film as a high‑energy experience first and a character study second. If you prioritize adrenaline‑pumping set pieces, let the chase scenes and shootouts lead your enjoyment. If you seek narrative depth, temper expectations and focus on the moments where the movie hints at broader social commentary. Discuss the film with friends to unpack how the emotional beats intersect with the action, and decide whether the trade‑off feels worthwhile for you.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t go in expecting fully fleshed‑out character arcs; the film leans heavily on style and momentum over deep development.</Callout>
