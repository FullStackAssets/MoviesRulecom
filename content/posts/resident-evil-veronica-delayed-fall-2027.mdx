Capcom’s highly anticipated Resident Evil Veronica remake, first hinted at during Summer Game Fest, is now slated for a fall 2027 launch instead of the originally targeted early‑year window. Insider Dusk Golem reports that internal pressures forced the studio to push the title from Q1 to Q2 2027, and then again to the autumn months. The delay appears tied to feedback on the recently released Resident Evil Requiem expansion, prompting Capcom to address lingering issues before the remake’s debut. For fans and investors, the shift reshapes the franchise’s 2027 roadmap and could influence sales momentum.

<Callout type="takeaway">Resident Evil Veronica’s release has slipped to fall 2027 due to post‑launch feedback on the Requiem expansion.</Callout>

## What happened
Capcom never announced an exact date for Veronica, but the studio originally aimed for a Q1 2027 release. According to insider Dusk Golem, the project was first moved to Q2 2027 as development faced intense pressure, and it has since been delayed again to the fall of 2027. The postponement also explains why a completed trailer has not been publicly shown.

The delay is linked to the Resident Evil Requiem expansion, whose post‑launch feedback revealed minor bugs and balance issues that Capcom chose to fix before finalizing Veronica. By addressing these concerns now, the company hopes to avoid cascading problems in the remake.

While a further push into 2028 remains possible, no official statement has confirmed a new timeline beyond the fall window.

## Why it matters
The shift affects fan expectations, as many were counting on a Q1 launch to coincide with other franchise events. Delaying the remake also impacts Capcom’s revenue projections for 2027, potentially shifting cash flow toward later quarters. Moreover, the decision signals that Capcom prioritizes quality and brand reputation over strict schedule adherence, a stance that could influence how future titles are timed.
