Quentin Tarantino’s 2015 revisionist Western, The Hateful Eight, is set to disappear from Tubi’s free streaming catalog this December. The removal marks the end of a four‑year run that gave fans easy access to the director’s follow‑up to Django Unchained. With the title leaving the platform, viewers will need to seek alternative services or purchase options to watch the film. The shift underscores how licensing windows can abruptly change the availability of cult‑favorite movies.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Hateful Eight will no longer be free on Tubi after December 2026, pushing viewers to other platforms.</Callout>

## What happened
Tubi announced that The Hateful Eight will be removed from its free tier effective December 2026, ending its current streaming availability. The film had been part of Tubi’s catalog for several years, offering viewers a no‑cost way to see Tarantino’s western sequel to Django Unchained.

The movie, released in 2015, was shot on 70 mm film with vintage lenses and serves as Tarantino’s homage to spaghetti westerns and locked‑room mysteries. Its predecessor, Django Unchained, grossed roughly $450 million worldwide against a reported $100 million budget, establishing a high commercial benchmark for the follow‑up.

## Why it matters
The loss of a free‑streaming home for The Hateful Eight highlights the fleeting nature of licensing agreements on ad‑supported platforms. Fans who discovered the film on Tubi now face a gap that may drive them to paid services or physical media, affecting both viewer habits and revenue streams for rights holders. Additionally, the removal may renew interest in the film’s unique production choices, such as its 70 mm format, as audiences search for alternative ways to experience it.
