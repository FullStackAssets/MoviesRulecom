The streaming giant is shifting its flagship focus from the sci‑fi hit Stranger Things to a sprawling fantasy epic: Netflix’s live‑action adaptation of One Piece.  After a three‑year wait, season 2 dropped on March 10, and the studio has already announced season 3, “The Battle of Alabasta,” for 2027.  With a plan to release new seasons annually, the series could run for ten seasons or more, potentially overtaking Stranger Things’ five‑season run that drew 133.8 million viewers in its first 91 days for season 5.  The move signals Netflix’s confidence in the manga’s vast narrative and its global fanbase.

<Callout type="takeaway">One Piece’s expansive source material and annual schedule position it to eclipse Stranger Things as Netflix’s flagship franchise.</Callout>

## What happened
Stranger Things has dominated Netflix’s all‑time TV top‑10, with season 4 ranking third at 140.7 million views and season 5 at fourth with 133.8 million.  The show launched careers for actors like Millie Bobby Brown and Finn Wolfhard and boosted veteran stars such as David Harbour.  Meanwhile, the One Piece adaptation released season 2 on March 10 after a three‑year gap, rekindling interest in the long‑running manga.  Netflix announced that season 3 will premiere in 2027, aiming for an annual release cadence and a potential 10‑season arc.

## Why it matters
The shift reflects Netflix’s strategy to secure long‑term, high‑budget franchises that can keep subscribers engaged over many years.  One Piece’s 100‑plus‑volume canon offers endless storylines, reducing the risk of narrative fatigue that plagued some earlier originals.  For fans, the promise of yearly seasons means more consistent content, while competitors may feel pressure to develop similarly ambitious series.  Economically, a 10‑season run could generate sustained revenue and merchandising opportunities comparable to the Stranger Things brand.
