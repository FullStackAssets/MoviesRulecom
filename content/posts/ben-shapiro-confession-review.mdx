Ben Shapiro’s first foray into screenwriting landed on the Daily Wire this week with the release of Confession, a period drama set in Stalinist Moscow. The film streams exclusively on the conservative outlet’s platform and boasts a surprisingly high‑profile cast, including Rob Lowe, James Franco, and Jennifer Jason Leigh. While the premise follows a professor’s interrogation in the Lubyanka prison, the movie also signals the Daily Wire’s growing ambition to produce feature‑length content. For viewers and industry watchers alike, the debut raises questions about the intersection of politics, streaming, and traditional filmmaking.

<Callout type="takeaway">Confession marks the Daily Wire’s most star‑studded original film yet, and its reception could shape future political‑driven streaming projects.</Callout>

## What happened
The Daily Wire released Confession on its own streaming site, positioning the film as its first feature written by Ben Shapiro, a prominent conservative commentator. The story follows a university professor, played by Rob Lowe, who is hauled to Moscow’s infamous Lubyanka Prison in 1938 for a brutal interrogation by his former student, portrayed by James Franco. Supporting roles are filled by Jennifer Jason Leigh, Brad Dourif, Nick Nolte, Kodi Smit‑McPhee and Emile Hirsch, giving the production a level of name recognition uncommon for the outlet’s previous releases.

Production was overseen by the Daily Wire’s media division, with Shapiro credited as sole screenwriter and Rob Lowe also serving as an executive producer. The film’s historical setting—a Stalinist purge—allows the creators to explore themes of state power and personal guilt, while also providing a showcase for the platform’s growing investment in original, high‑budget content.

## Why it matters
Confession represents a strategic shift for the Daily Wire, moving from short‑form video to full‑length cinema. By attaching recognizable talent, the outlet hopes to attract viewers beyond its core political audience and to compete with mainstream streaming services that are expanding their own original film slates. The project also illustrates how political media brands are leveraging narrative filmmaking to reinforce ideological messages, potentially influencing how future political content is packaged and consumed.

## How to think about it
Approach Confession as both a piece of entertainment and a case study in media branding. Evaluate the film on its acting, direction, and production design before weighing its political framing.
