The Netflix series *The Empress* ends its second season with Emperor Franz Joseph marching to the front lines against Napoleon III, leaving viewers wondering about his ultimate fate. In reality, the Habsburg ruler did join the Austrian army during the Second Italian War of Independence and commanded forces at the Battle of Solferino, a decisive clash that ended in defeat for Austria. While the show dramatizes his motivations, the core event—Franz Joseph’s presence on the battlefield—is historically accurate. Decades later, the emperor died of natural causes, far removed from the war‑torn climax portrayed on screen.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Empress captures a pivotal wartime moment, but Franz Joseph’s later life and death diverge from the series’ cliffhanger.</Callout>

## What happened
The series depicts Franz Joseph leaving Vienna to fight alongside his troops, a scene that mirrors his real‑life decision to lead Austrian forces against Napoleon III’s army. Historical accounts confirm his involvement in the Second Italian War of Independence and his command at Solferino, where Austria suffered a significant loss that reshaped the Italian unification process.

After the war, Franz Joseph continued to reign for many decades, overseeing a vast empire that survived into the early twentieth century. He eventually passed away from natural causes, a quiet end compared to the dramatic battlefield exit shown in the series.

Understanding the distinction helps audiences appreciate both the entertainment value and the historical significance.
