Fans of off‑beat crime cinema have a new reason to revisit the late‑90s cult classic “Go”. The film, which debuted in April 1999 and featured Timothy Olyphant alongside an ensemble cast, has just been added to Pluto TV’s free, ad‑supported library. Unlike many older titles that disappear behind paywalls, “Go” can now be streamed at no cost, exposing its Tarantino‑meets‑Ritchie storytelling to a fresh generation. The move also highlights Pluto TV’s strategy of bolstering its catalog with recognizable, under‑the‑radar movies.

## What happened
The darkly comic crime thriller, directed by Doug Liman and written by John August, premiered in April 1999 with a cast that included Sarah Polley, Katie Holmes, Desmond Askew, Taye Diggs, Jay Mohr, Scott Wolf and Timothy Olyphant. Its non‑linear narrative, interweaving three storylines around a Christmas backdrop, drew comparisons to Quentin Tarantino’s “Pulp Fiction” and Guy Ritchie’s early British capers.

More than 25 years after its original release, the movie has been added to Pluto TV’s growing free library. The ad‑supported platform offers the film at no charge, allowing viewers who missed it the first time around to watch it without a subscription.

## Why it matters
Placing “Go” on a free, ad‑supported service underscores a broader shift toward making niche, cult‑status titles accessible without paywalls. For Pluto TV, the addition diversifies its catalog and leverages name‑recognizable talent to attract viewers seeking retro crime fare. For audiences, the move revives a film that helped define late‑90s indie crime storytelling, offering a low‑friction way to explore its Tarantino‑style dialogue and Ritchie‑esque pacing.
