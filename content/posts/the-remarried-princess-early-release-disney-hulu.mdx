Disney+ and Hulu are set to surprise K‑drama fans on November 3 by releasing the first four episodes of ‘The Remarried Princess’ a day ahead of schedule. The move follows a press conference in Seoul that framed the early drop as a thank‑you to viewers after the series’ trailer amassed over 13 million views in its first 48 hours. The show, adapted from a wildly popular Naver webtoon with nearly three billion cumulative reads, stars Shin Mina and Ju Jihoon in a tale of royal intrigue. By launching early, the streaming platforms hope to capitalize on the buzz and keep the momentum through a staggered weekly release. The early release also signals a broader strategy of using fan‑driven incentives to boost subscriber engagement.

<Callout type="takeaway">The early drop rewards fan excitement and gives Disney+ and Hulu a competitive edge in the K‑drama market.</Callout>

## What happened
The first four episodes of “The Remarried Princess” will become available on Tuesday, November 3, on both Disney+ and Hulu, one day before the originally planned November 4 premiere. Disney+ and Hulu announced the shift during a live press conference in Seoul, describing it as a special thank‑you to fans for their overwhelming support.

The series’ promotional trailer broke 13 million views within 48 hours, fueling anticipation that was further amplified by sold‑out preview screenings at the Busan International Film Festival. The drama adapts the hit Naver webtoon, which has accumulated close to three billion views, and follows Empress Navier (Shin Mina) as she navigates a fraught marriage and a politically charged remarriage.

After the initial four‑episode launch, two new episodes will drop every Wednesday from November 11 through the finale on November 25, keeping viewers engaged over a three‑week span.

## Why it matters
Releasing the opening block early gives Disney+ and Hulu a clear differentiator in the crowded streaming landscape, especially as K‑drama content becomes a major draw for global audiences. The strategy leverages real‑time fan enthusiasm, turning social‑media momentum into immediate subscriber value. It also demonstrates how streaming services can use flexible scheduling to respond to audience demand, potentially reshaping release models for future international adaptations.
