The long‑running procedural Criminal Minds is officially ending. Paramount+ announced that the series will conclude with its 20th season, the final chapter of the BAU saga that began on CBS in 2006. The decision was made under the new Skydance Direct‑to‑Consumer leadership, although earlier recommendations to wrap up the show came from the previous Paramount+ team. Fans will see the BAU’s last case this fall, marking the close of one of television’s most enduring crime dramas. The cancellation reshapes the streaming landscape for procedural fans.

<Callout type="takeaway">Criminal Minds will end after its 20th season, closing a two‑decade run on television.</Callout>

## What happened
Paramount+ confirmed that Criminal Minds will not be renewed beyond season 20, effectively ending the series after more than two decades on air. The cancellation was reported by Deadline and attributed to the new leadership team headed by Casey Bloys at Skydance Direct‑to‑Consumer, who finalized the decision despite earlier internal recommendations to conclude the show.

The series, which launched on CBS in 2006, transitioned to Paramount+ and was rebranded as Criminal Minds: Evolution for its final five seasons. Season 20 will be the last installment, wrapping up the Behavioral Analysis Unit’s investigations and providing a final farewell to its ensemble cast.

## Why it matters
The end of Criminal Minds removes one of the longest‑running procedural anchors from both broadcast and streaming lineups, potentially affecting subscriber retention for Paramount+. The show's departure also signals a shift toward newer, perhaps more experimental crime dramas as the platform seeks fresh content to attract viewers. For fans, the cancellation closes a cultural touchstone that has influenced countless spin‑offs and inspired a generation of crime‑show enthusiasts.
