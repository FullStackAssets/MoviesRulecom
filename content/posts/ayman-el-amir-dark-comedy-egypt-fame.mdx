The Cannes‑winning documentary filmmaker Ayman El Amir is moving from nonfiction to satire with his next feature, “A Pair of Shoes in the Dark Corner of the Moon.” The film follows George, a Coptic dreamer from a remote Egyptian village who lands a fleeting spot on a space‑themed reality show and is briefly hailed as a national hero. When the show collapses as a scam, George returns home to find the promised sewage mega‑project was also an illusion, prompting a journey of self‑discovery. El Amir’s project, selected for the Crossroads Co‑Production Forum at the Thessaloniki International Film Festival, signals a bold critique of Egypt’s current fame‑obsessed culture.

<Callout type="takeaway">El Amir uses dark comedy to expose Egypt’s feverish chase for fame and its hollow promises.</Callout>

## What happened
Ayman El Amir, who co‑directed the Cannes Golden Eye‑winning documentary “The Brink of Dreams,” announced that he has written and directed a new satirical feature titled “A Pair of Shoes in the Dark Corner of the Moon.” The film is produced by Nada Riyadh under their Cairo‑based company Felucca Films and will be part of the Crossroads Co‑Production Forum, the industry arm of the Thessaloniki International Film Festival.

Set in a remote village, the story centers on George, a quirky Coptic actor who dreams of stardom while working as an extra in low‑budget productions. A promised sewage mega‑project brings a space‑themed reality TV show to the village, and George wins a spot that catapults him to national fame. The show is later exposed as a fraud, leaving George disgraced; he discovers the village’s infrastructure promise was also an illusion and embarks on a personal quest for identity and belonging.

## Why it matters
The film tackles a precarious moment in Egyptian history marked by unregulated capitalism and grand state projects that often fail to materialize. By framing the narrative as a dark comedy, El Amir highlights the societal pressure to achieve fame at any cost and the disillusionment that follows hollow promises, offering a cultural mirror for audiences both inside and outside Egypt.
