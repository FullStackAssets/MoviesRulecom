Mike Flanagan, the creator of Prime Video’s reimagined “Carrie,” sat down with Deadline to reflect on the series’ first season. He explained how the show uses body‑horror and social‑media cruelty to update Stephen King’s classic for today’s teenagers. Flanagan also revealed that he is now attached to a Warhammer 40,000 series, describing the project as “the time of my life” with a “very cool and unexpected on‑ramp.” Both projects signal Amazon MGM Studios’ aggressive push into young‑adult and genre‑bending content. The interview offers insight into how horror can comment on modern teen culture while expanding into a massive sci‑fi franchise.
<Callout type="takeaway">Flanagan sees horror as a mirror for teen cruelty and treats Warhammer as a fresh, unexpected creative launchpad.</Callout>
## What happened
In the Deadline interview, Flanagan said the first season of “Carrie” was designed to explore the choices between kindness and cruelty that teenagers face, tying bullying scandals to the protagonist’s telekinetic powers. He noted that the series deliberately re‑imagines the title character as an “anti‑Carrie,” a more optimistic teen confronting public shaming on social media. Flanagan also talked about his upcoming work on a Warhammer 40,000 series, calling the opportunity “the time of my life” and highlighting an “unexpected on‑ramp” that will bring the grimdark universe to a broader audience.
## Why it matters
The revival of “Carrie” shows how classic horror can be repurposed for YA audiences, reinforcing Prime Video’s strategy to dominate the streaming teen‑horror niche. Flanagan’s emphasis on cruelty and social media resonates with viewers who experience digital bullying, making the show culturally relevant. Meanwhile, his involvement in a Warhammer 40,000 adaptation signals Amazon’s intent to diversify its portfolio beyond contemporary drama into high‑budget sci‑fi, potentially attracting a different fan base and expanding the franchise’s reach.
