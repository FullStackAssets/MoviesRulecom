Netflix has just confirmed that its adult‑animated Twilight reboot, Midnight Sun, will launch with a two‑season order. The announcement came during Lionsgate’s quarterly earnings call, where the studio revealed the deal with Netflix and highlighted the series’ potential to expand Stephenie Meyer’s universe. This move signals a major investment in a franchise that has already generated over $3.3 billion worldwide, despite mixed critical reception. Fans and investors alike are watching to see how the animated format will reinterpret the beloved story for an adult audience. The confirmation also sets expectations for a longer narrative arc than the original film series.

## What happened
Netflix and Lionsgate disclosed that the adult‑animated series Midnight Sun has been ordered for two seasons on Netflix. The series reimagines the Twilight saga in an animated format aimed at mature viewers, with creator Stephanie Meyer (or team) closely involved in the production process. Lionsgate emphasized high expectations and hopes for audience engagement, noting that the order reflects confidence in the franchise’s enduring popularity.

The original Twilight film franchise, launched in 2008, amassed more than $3.3 billion in global box office receipts despite largely negative critical reviews. By moving to an adult‑animated series, Netflix aims to tap both nostalgic fans and new viewers seeking a darker, more nuanced take on the story.

## Why it matters
The two‑season order signals Netflix’s strategy to leverage established IPs with fresh formats, reducing risk while promising longer subscriber retention. For the Twilight fanbase, the animated series offers a chance to explore deeper character arcs and themes that the films could not fully address. Industry observers see this as a test case for how legacy franchises can be revitalized on streaming platforms, potentially influencing future deals for other beloved properties.
