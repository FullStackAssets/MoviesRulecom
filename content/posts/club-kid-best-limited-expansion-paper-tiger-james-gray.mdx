Club Kid’s second‑week run delivered $756,000 on just 27 screens, making it the strongest limited expansion of 2026 in the specialty market. The film attracted an audience that was 80 % under 35, underscoring the growing appetite for fresh, youth‑driven indie fare. At the same time, James Gray’s new thriller Paper Tiger opened on six screens in New York and Los Angeles and posted a $35,800 per‑screen average, the highest of his career. Both results highlight a shift toward smaller‑scale releases that can generate outsized buzz and revenue. For distributors and exhibitors, the data offers a blueprint for targeting demographics and maximizing limited‑screen strategies.

<Callout type="takeaway">Limited‑screen releases can outperform wider films when they hit the right audience.</Callout>

## What happened
Club Kid, an A24‑distributed feature debut by Jordan Firstman, earned $756k in its second week on only 27 screens, delivering the best limited‑expansion gross of the year. The audience skewed heavily younger, with 80 % of viewers under 35, and the film’s cumulative total reached $1.45 million, matching the performance of comparable indie titles like Anora.

Paper Tiger, James Gray’s Neon‑shot crime thriller starring Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson and Miles Teller, opened on six screens in New York and Los Angeles and generated a $35.8k per‑screen average for a $215k opening gross. The launch included sold‑out director Q&A events, further amplifying word‑of‑mouth momentum.

## Why it matters
The two successes illustrate how targeted, limited releases can capture niche audiences and still deliver headline‑making numbers. A strong youth turnout for Club Kid signals that younger viewers are increasingly driving specialty box‑office performance, which could reshape acquisition strategies for indie distributors. Paper Tiger’s per‑screen strength demonstrates that high‑profile talent paired with a limited rollout can yield a premium average, giving filmmakers leverage for future negotiations and wider releases.

## How to think about it
When evaluating an indie title, start by mapping its core demographic and assess whether a limited‑screen strategy can concentrate that audience in key markets. Pair the rollout with event programming—such as director Q&As or themed screenings—to amplify buzz. Track per‑screen averages closely; strong numbers can be leveraged to negotiate additional screens or streaming deals. Finally, use the early‑week data to decide if a gradual expansion aligns with the film’s momentum rather than a blanket wide release.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that a strong limited opening guarantees success on a larger scale; each film’s genre, star power, and market conditions differ.</Callout>
