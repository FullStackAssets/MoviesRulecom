Screen Rant recently published a list that ranks the ten most formidable Marvel Cinematic Universe villains based on how close they came to achieving their ultimate objectives. The article evaluates each antagonist’s plan, the extent of its success, the effort required to reverse it, and whether any victory endured beyond the final frame. From Loki’s near‑world‑conquest in *The Avengers* to Thanos’s half‑universe snap, the ranking spotlights moments when defeat was anything but certain. Understanding these near‑wins adds a new layer to how fans appreciate the stakes of each MCU film.

<Callout type="takeaway">The closer a villain gets to their goal, the more it reveals the MCU’s narrative tension.</Callout>

## What happened
Screen Rant’s editorial team compiled the list by weighing three criteria: how much of each villain’s plan succeeded, what it took for heroes to reverse the damage, and whether any triumph lasted after the credits rolled. The ranking draws exclusively from theatrical MCU releases, deliberately excluding TV‑only antagonists to keep the focus on big‑screen storytelling.

Key entries include Loki, whose Chitauri invasion in *The Avengers* briefly threatened global domination before the portal was shut; Adrian Toomes (the Vulture) in *Spider‑Man: Homecoming*, whose weapon‑selling scheme nearly reshaped the illegal arms market; Thanos, whose snap eliminated half of all life and required the combined effort of the Avengers to undo; and Helmut Zemo, whose plan to fracture the Avengers from within succeeded in sowing lasting distrust. Each entry is measured against the villain’s own objective, whether it was world conquest, personal profit, or ideological destruction.

## Why it matters
Villains drive the emotional core of superhero narratives, and a near‑victory amplifies the tension that makes a film memorable. By quantifying how close each antagonist came to winning, the list invites fans to re‑examine plot twists and appreciate the thin line between triumph and defeat. It also highlights how the MCU balances heroic resilience with credible threats, reinforcing why certain movies stand out in the decade‑long saga.

## How to think about it
Use the ranking as a guide for themed marathon viewing: start with the villains who almost won, then watch the corresponding hero films to see how the narrative restores balance. When discussing a movie, consider the villain’s objective and ask whether the heroes truly prevented a lasting change or merely delayed it. This framework can deepen conversations on character motivation and plot structure across the MCU.
