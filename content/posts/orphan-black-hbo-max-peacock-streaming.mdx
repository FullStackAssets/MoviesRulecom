Orphan Black, the groundbreaking series that cemented Tatiana Maslany’s place as a television chameleon, has found a new home. HBO Max and Peacock have each secured the rights to stream all five seasons, ending its year‑long stint on Netflix. The move expands the show’s reach to subscribers of two major platforms, reviving interest in a series praised for its complex narrative and clone‑driven drama. With Maslany portraying 17 distinct clones and earning an Emmy win, the series remains a benchmark for sci‑fi storytelling. Fans and newcomers alike can now explore the thriller that many critics label the 21st century’s best.

<Callout type="takeaway">Orphan Black’s shift to HBO Max and Peacock puts the era‑defining sci‑fi thriller back in the spotlight for a broader streaming audience.</Callout>

## What happened
All five seasons of Orphan Black are now available on HBO Max and Peacock after Boat Rocker Studios finalized new U.S. distribution deals. The series originally moved to Netflix on September 1 2025 and left the platform on August 31 2026, completing a twelve‑month window.

The show follows clone Sarah Manning, who discovers she is one of many genetically identical individuals, a premise that unfolds over five seasons. Maslany’s performance earned three Emmy nominations and one win, highlighting the series’ critical acclaim.

## Why it matters
The migration signals a strategic push by premium streaming services to acquire prestige‑television properties that attract dedicated fanbases. By hosting Orphan Black, HBO Max and Peacock can differentiate their libraries with a series that blends thriller pacing with speculative science, potentially drawing viewers who appreciate shows like Severance or Black Mirror. The renewed visibility also encourages new discussions around cloning ethics, identity, and representation in media.
