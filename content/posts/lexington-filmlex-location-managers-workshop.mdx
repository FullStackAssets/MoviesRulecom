Lexington’s FilmLEX office has taken a bold step to nurture homegrown talent by teaming up with the Location Managers Guild International (LMGI). The two‑day workshop, "From Page to Place: The Art of Location Management," runs Nov. 6‑7 and welcomes students, emerging crew and local professionals. Industry veterans such as Andrea Keener, Todd Christensen, Mike Fantasia and Dennis Muscari will lead sessions on scouting, logistics and on‑set problem solving. Over 200 participants from eight Kentucky colleges, universities and area high schools are slated to attend. The initiative aims to cement Lexington as a production‑ready hub and create a pipeline for future jobs.
<Callout type="takeaway">Lexington is building a sustainable local film workforce by linking students directly with seasoned location managers.</Callout>
## What happened
The Lexington Film Office, operating as FilmLEX, announced a partnership with the Location Managers Guild International to deliver a workforce development program titled "From Page to Place: The Art of Location Management." The two‑day event is scheduled for Nov. 6‑7 at the FilmLEX headquarters.
The curriculum features hands‑on training, mentorship and networking, with LMGI professionals Andrea Keener (Killers of the Flower Moon, Captain America: Brave New World), Todd Christensen (The Hunger Games, Sicario), Mike Fantasia (Top Gun: Maverick, Killers of the Flower Moon) and Dennis Muscari (Oppenheimer, Breaking Bad) leading the sessions. Students from eight regional colleges—including the University of Kentucky, Asbury University and Transylvania University—along with area high schools will take part.
Lisa Brin, head of FilmLEX, called the partnership a “tremendous privilege” and a first step toward strengthening the local production workforce. Danny Finn, president of LMGI, added that investing in the next generation is essential for the industry’s future.
## Why it matters
By giving aspiring location managers direct access to seasoned professionals, Lexington is cultivating a talent pipeline that can reduce reliance on out‑of‑state crews and lower production costs. A stronger local workforce makes the city more attractive to film and TV projects, which can generate jobs, tax revenue and cultural visibility. The initiative also signals to other mid‑size markets that targeted training can be a catalyst for sustainable industry growth.

## How to think about it
If you are a student, treat the workshop as both a learning experience and a credential—follow up with mentors and seek internships. Local producers should monitor the alumni list and consider hiring workshop graduates for entry‑level location roles, saving on travel and housing costs for out‑of‑state crews. Film offices in other regions can replicate this model by partnering with guilds to create short, intensive training that aligns with local production needs.
<Callout type="warning">Attendance does not guarantee employment; participants must continue building experience and connections after the workshop.</Callout>
