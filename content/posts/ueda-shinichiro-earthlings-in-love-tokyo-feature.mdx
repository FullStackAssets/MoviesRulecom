Director Ueda Shinichiro, celebrated for the self‑reflexive zombie hit One Cut of the Dead, is turning his latest social‑media microdrama into a theatrical feature. The new film, Earthlings in Love, will debut in the Nippon Cinema Now section of the 39th Tokyo International Film Festival. The story follows a director with young‑onset dementia who revisits a teenage‑made film to preserve his memories. By expanding a series of 30 vertical short episodes into a full‑length picture, Ueda is testing how internet‑born formats can survive on the big screen. The experiment could reshape how filmmakers bridge digital and cinematic storytelling.

<Callout type="takeaway">Ueda’s vertical microdrama proves that social‑media formats can be re‑imagined as feature‑length cinema.</Callout>

## What happened
Ueda Shinichiro’s Earthlings in Love originated as a collection of thirty short episodes, each only a few minutes long, released vertically on social platforms. An enthusiastic online response prompted him to adapt the material into a feature‑length film for theatrical exhibition.

The narrative centers on Shinpei, a director diagnosed with young‑onset dementia, who decides to record his memories by finishing a film he began with friends as a teenager. The project was conceived with vertical framing before the screenplay was written, allowing the visual language of the original microdrama to inform the longer structure.

## Why it matters
The transition from bite‑size, platform‑specific content to a conventional cinema format challenges the industry’s assumptions about audience expectations and distribution. If successful, it could open a pathway for other creators to leverage viral short‑form storytelling into mainstream releases, expanding the economic and artistic viability of vertical video.

## How to think about it
Treat the vertical format as a storytelling constraint rather than a limitation. Identify the core emotional beats that worked in the short episodes and expand them with subplots and character development that suit a 90‑minute runtime. When presenting the film in traditional venues, consider supplemental framing or slight re‑composition to ease audience adaptation while preserving the original aesthetic.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the vertical format will automatically engage theater audiences; test screen the film in both vertical and standard orientations before wide release.</Callout>
