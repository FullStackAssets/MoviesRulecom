Looking for a weekend binge that satisfies both curiosity and entertainment? Netflix’s November slate drops three documentaries that span a violent cartel empire, the rise of a legendary Canadian sketch show, and a perplexing missing‑person mystery. Each offers a deep dive into its subject, making them perfect for marathon viewing. Whether you’re a true‑crime aficionado, a comedy history buff, or a mystery lover, these titles have something to hook you.

<Callout type="takeaway">Netflix’s November documentary lineup offers a diverse binge‑worthy mix of true‑crime, comedy history, and unsolved mystery.</Callout>

## What happened
The Fall of El Mayo is a three‑part series that follows the shadowy rise and eventual downfall of Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada, the co‑founder of the Sinaloa Cartel. By focusing on the figure who operated behind the scenes while his partner Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán became the public face, the documentary sheds light on the cartel’s internal dynamics and its impact on Mexican politics.

SCTV: The Greatest Show You’ve Never Heard Of chronicles the Canadian sketch‑comedy series that launched the careers of icons like John Candy and Eugene Levy. Through interviews and archival footage, the film explores how the low‑budget program influenced North‑American comedy and why it remains a cult favorite.

The third documentary tackles a famous missing‑person case that continues to generate debate. While the series does not name the subject directly, it revisits the investigation, presents new interviews, and examines the lingering questions that keep the public engaged.

## Why it matters
These three titles illustrate Netflix’s commitment to varied nonfiction storytelling, catering to audiences who crave both education and entertainment. The cartel series offers insight into ongoing drug‑war debates, the SCTV film preserves an overlooked cultural milestone, and the missing‑person documentary reminds viewers of the power of investigative media to keep cold cases in the public eye.
