When the Tokyo International Film Festival announced its competition lineup, Chinese artist Sun Xun’s “Magic Atlas” stole the spotlight as the first animated feature ever selected. The decade‑long project blends woodblock prints, ink painting and vivid color to tell a story set in a coal‑mining empire where flying is forbidden. Its inclusion marks a shift for the festival, which has traditionally showcased live‑action cinema. Sun sees the film as his “all‑in” moment, a work that demanded every personal and financial sacrifice. The selection signals new possibilities for hybrid art‑film projects on the global stage.
<Callout type="takeaway">Magic Atlas proves that boundary‑pushing animation can compete alongside live‑action cinema at major festivals.</Callout>
## What happened
Tokyo International Film Festival confirmed that “Magic Atlas” will compete in the main slate, making it the festival’s inaugural animated title in competition. Sun Xun, known for his experimental art and filmmaking, told Variety the film was never intended as a conventional cartoon, but as a fusion of multiple art forms.
The film, set in the fictional Luocha empire, follows a magician who gives an orphan a map that reveals a erased past. Production spanned ten years; Sun invested his savings, sourced buyers during pandemic‑closed galleries, and took loans to finish the project.
## Why it matters
The selection challenges the traditional live‑action bias of major festivals and validates animation as a medium for serious, art‑driven storytelling. It also demonstrates that long‑term, self‑financed projects can achieve high‑profile exposure, encouraging other independent creators to pursue ambitious visions despite financial hurdles. For audiences, it expands the range of cinematic experiences available at prestigious events.

## How to think about it
Creators aiming for festival recognition should consider blending traditional art techniques with animation to stand out visually, while building a diversified funding plan that doesn’t rely solely on personal savings. Engaging directly with galleries, collectors, and online platforms can generate early revenue streams. When pitching to festivals, emphasize the work’s artistic intent and cultural relevance to align with curatorial goals.
<Callout type="warning">Don’t assume that personal savings and loans are a sustainable model for most creators; each project’s financial risk varies.</Callout>
