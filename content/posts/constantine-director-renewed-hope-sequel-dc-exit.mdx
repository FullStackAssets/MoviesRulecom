Francis Lawrence, the director of the 2005 'Constantine' film, told Collider he now feels 'renewed hope' for a sequel. His optimism follows the abrupt exit of James Gunn and Peter Safran from DC Studios, a shift that could reshape the studio's slate. Lawrence said the previous DC leadership had been an impediment, but the new regime may finally allow his long‑standing project to move forward. The potential sequel matters because it would revive a cult‑favorite property and test how the new DC leadership balances legacy films with upcoming blockbusters.

<Callout type="takeaway">Lawrence sees the DC leadership change as a possible green light for Constantine 2.</Callout>

## What happened

Lawrence explained that he has a script and an idea that have been waiting for over 20 years, and that collaborators like J.J. Abrams are attached. He noted that obstacles were not his or Keanu Reeves' fault but stemmed from people at DC.

At the same time, DC Studios had announced a separate Hellblazer adaptation by Francis Galluppi, but the project's status is uncertain after Gunn and Safran's departure. New head Jeremy Latcham is expected to prioritize titles such as Wonder Woman and The Flash.

## Why it matters

A Constantine sequel would tap into an established fan base and demonstrate the new DC leadership's willingness to invest in legacy properties, while also influencing how the studio balances standalone films with a broader shared universe. Its success or failure could set a precedent for other dormant franchises.
