Olivia Cooke, fresh from her acclaimed turn on Slow Horses, headlines the R‑rated thriller A Talent for Murder, which just landed on Rotten Tomatoes with a 79% audience score. The film, directed by Anton Corbijn and written by Joanne Murray‑Smith, has already earned a Certified Fresh 78% from critics based on 60 reviews. Its audience rating is drawn from more than 50 viewer votes, indicating early enthusiasm that mirrors critical consensus. For fans of Cooke and the Slow Horses ensemble, the strong reception suggests the actress is successfully expanding beyond the spy series. The numbers matter because they signal that an R‑rated, literary‑inspired thriller can attract both critics and general viewers.
<Callout type="takeaway">The film’s audience score mirrors its critic rating, showing broad appeal despite an R rating.</Callout>
## What happened
A Talent for Murder debuted on Rotten Tomatoes with a 79% audience score, compiled from over 50 audience ratings at the time of reporting. Critics responded positively as well, granting the film a Certified Fresh 78% based on 60 reviews. The movie follows literary editor Edward Ridgeway (Alden Ehrenreich) on a perilous trip to Switzerland to persuade author Patricia Highsmith (Helen Mirren) to write another Tom Ripley novel, while Cooke portrays Clementine Balfour, an American heiress and Edward’s love interest.
The project brings together veteran talent: director Anton Corbijn, known for his visual storytelling, and a cast that includes Helen Mirren alongside Cooke. The film’s R rating underscores its mature themes and intense suspense, distinguishing it from the more mainstream entries in Cooke’s recent filmography.
For context, the Slow Horses series—where Cooke plays Sid Baker—holds a 97% critic score and 87% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, underscoring the actor’s track record of choosing well‑received projects.
## Why it matters
The near‑parity between audience and critic scores suggests the film resonates across viewer segments, a notable achievement for an R‑rated indie thriller. This alignment can boost confidence among distributors and streaming platforms considering acquisition, potentially expanding the film’s reach beyond limited theatrical runs. Cooke’s rising profile, reinforced by strong reception, may open doors for more leading roles in genre‑bending projects. Additionally, the positive metrics challenge the notion that R‑rated titles struggle to find broad appeal in a market dominated by PG‑13 blockbusters.
