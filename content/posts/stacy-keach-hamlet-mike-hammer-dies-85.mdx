Stacy Keach, the versatile actor best known for his stage Hamlet and the TV detective Mike Hammer, died on Tuesday at age 85. He passed away at his family home in Magdalenka, Poland, after a prolonged battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure. Keach’s five‑decade career spanned Broadway, television, film, and voice work, making him a distinctive presence in American entertainment. His death marks the loss of a performer who consistently navigated diverse roles and left an indelible imprint on multiple generations of audiences.

## What happened
Stacy Keach died at his family’s home in Magdalenka, Poland, following a lengthy struggle with a serious heart condition that culminated in heart failure. His family announced the news, noting his age of 85 and the private nature of his final days.

Keach built a career that blended classical theater with popular television, most famously embodying Hamlet on Broadway and Mike Hammer in the 1980s series. He also lent his voice to PBS’s “Nova” and the true‑crime series “American Greed,” while appearing in films such as “The New Centurions,” “Fat City,” and the Cheech & Chong comedies “Up in Smoke” and “Nice Dreams.”

Despite his extensive résumé, Keach missed several high‑profile film roles, including Father Damien Karras in “The Exorcist” and R.P. McMurphy in “One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest,” setbacks that he later reflected on in his memoirs.
