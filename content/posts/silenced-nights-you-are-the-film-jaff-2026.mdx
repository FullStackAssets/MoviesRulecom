The 21st Jogja‑Netpac Asian Film Festival (JAFF) kicks off in Yogyakarta on Nov 28, spotlighting a Filipino drama and a Japanese comedy‑drama as bookends. Lawrence Fajardo’s “Silenced Nights” opens the slate, bringing its Toronto‑premiered story to an Indonesian audience for the first time. The festival will close with Makoto Ueda’s “You Are the Film,” a recent White Raven Award winner from Brussels. With 254 films from 34 countries and a theme of “Influx,” the event promises to map shifting cultural currents across Asia.

<Callout type="takeaway">JAFF uses its opening and closing films to frame a festival‑wide conversation about change and perspective.</Callout>

## What happened
The festival runs Nov 28–Dec 5 in Yogyakarta under the theme “Influx,” screening 254 titles from 34 nations. It opens with the Filipino drama “Silenced Nights,” which debuted at Toronto, and closes with the Japanese comedy‑drama “You Are the Film,” a White Raven Award winner at Brussels.

Ten features compete for the Golden and Silver Hanoman Awards, judged by a jury that includes Dita Gambiro, Riar Rizaldi and Shan Zuolong. Among the contenders are first‑feature works by women filmmakers, such as China’s “A Girl Unknown” and Singapore’s “Ah Girl,” both recent festival award winners.

On the local front, ten Indonesian films vie in the Indonesian Screen Awards across categories ranging from Best Film to Best Director, highlighting homegrown talent alongside the international lineup.

## Why it matters
JAFF’s programming underscores the growing interconnectedness of Asian cinema, giving regional audiences access to stories that might otherwise be confined to festival circuits in the West. By pairing a socially resonant Filipino drama with a genre‑bending Japanese comedy, the festival illustrates how diverse narratives can illuminate the same theme of societal flux. The competition sections also provide a launchpad for emerging filmmakers, especially women, whose visibility can shift industry dynamics across the continent.
