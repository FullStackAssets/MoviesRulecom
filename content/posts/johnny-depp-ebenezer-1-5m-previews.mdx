Johnny Depp returns to the big screen with the holiday‑themed ghost story Ebenezer, opening in more than 3,500 North American theaters. Thursday’s preview screenings generated $1.5 million, a modest start for a film with a $38 million budget. The studio is projecting a $20 million domestic haul and a matching $20 million overseas, aiming for a $40 million worldwide opening. The numbers matter because they signal whether Depp can reclaim A‑list status after a turbulent few years. Critics are split, with a 52 percent Rotten Tomatoes rating, adding uncertainty to the box‑office outlook.

<Callout type="takeaway">Depp’s preview earnings hint at a cautious comeback, but the film still faces a steep climb to profitability.</Callout>

## What happened
Paramount Pictures released Ebenezer in over 3,500 North American theaters on Friday, and Thursday previews brought in $1.5 million. The $38 million‑budget adaptation of Charles Dickens’ A Christmas Carol, directed by Ti West, stars Depp as the miserly Scrooge alongside Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Sam Claflin, Daisy Ridley and Ian McKellen. Paramount projects a $20 million domestic total and an additional $20 million from 63 international markets, targeting a $40 million worldwide opening.

The release follows Depp’s last studio outing in 2018’s Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald, after which he was dropped by Warner Bros. amid a high‑profile defamation trial. Competing holiday titles include Amazon MGM’s How to Rob a Bank, which earned $900,000 in previews across 2,810 theaters, and Sony’s Ghost Soldier, which pulled $3.1 million from 2,558 locations over two days.

## Why it matters
Ebenezer serves as a litmus test for Depp’s ability to rebound from personal and professional setbacks, offering Paramount a potential anchor for the holiday season. A strong opening could restore confidence in Depp‑led projects and influence future casting decisions at major studios. Conversely, a tepid performance would reinforce doubts about his box‑office draw and could tighten the market for star‑driven holiday releases.

## How to think about it
For investors, monitor the film’s first‑weekend gross relative to its $1.5 M preview base; a significant lift suggests momentum, while flat growth signals trouble. Fans should weigh the novelty of Depp’s return against the mixed reviews and consider waiting for the streaming window if the theatrical experience feels uncertain. Marketers can capitalize on the holiday theme and the Dickens brand while highlighting Depp’s comeback narrative to attract both nostalgic viewers and new audiences.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the $1.5 M preview figure guarantees a $40 M opening; audience sentiment can shift after the first weekend.</Callout>
