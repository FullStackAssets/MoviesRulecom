California’s entertainment hub could get a massive infusion of money as Gov. Gavin Newsom unveiled a proposal to expand the state’s film and TV tax credit program to at least $1 billion. The plan adds above‑the‑line talent credits, earmarks $100 million for independent productions, and rewards studios that commit to multiple projects in the state. It also extends the credit’s expiration from its current date to 2040. Newsom is pairing the state effort with a push for Congress to pass the Motion Picture, Television and Entertainment Revitalization Act (MPTERA) by year‑end. If adopted, the expansion could reshape where movies and streaming series are filmed.

<Callout type="takeaway">The $1 billion expansion aims to keep California the primary destination for film and TV production.</Callout>

## What happened
In a statement released on November 2, 2026, Governor Newsom announced a proposal to increase California’s film and TV tax credit budget to a minimum of $1 billion annually. The proposal introduces credits for above‑the‑line talent such as directors and lead actors, allocates $100 million specifically for independent productions, and offers additional incentives to companies that schedule more than one production in the state.

The governor also urged the U.S. Congress to enact the Motion Picture, Television and Entertainment Revitalization Act (MPTERA) before the end of the year, arguing that a federal counterpart would share the financial burden and strengthen the national industry.

The initiative comes amid the 2026 midterm election cycle, as California prepares to elect Newsom’s successor, and follows a historic expansion of the credit program last year that attracted over $5 billion in qualified spending.

## Why it matters
An expanded credit could funnel billions of dollars of production spending back into California, preserving jobs that have been drifting to other states with more generous incentives. More on‑location filming means local crews, vendors, and post‑production facilities benefit, while streaming platforms gain easier access to California’s talent pool. However, the scale of the proposed funding also raises questions about the state’s fiscal capacity and the need for complementary federal support.

## How to think about it
Producers should monitor the legislative timeline and begin preliminary budgeting that incorporates potential credit amounts. Independent filmmakers can explore the dedicated $100 million pool as a viable financing source. Studios planning multiple projects should evaluate the multi‑production reward to maximize savings. Keeping an eye on MPTERA’s progress will also be crucial, as federal backing could affect eligibility and credit values.
