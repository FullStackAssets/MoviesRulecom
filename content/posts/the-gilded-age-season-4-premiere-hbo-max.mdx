HBO Max is set to roll out the highly anticipated fourth season of The Gilded Age this Sunday, November 1. The series returns with its signature old‑money power struggles, now amplified by fresh rivalries and the fallout from Bertha Russell’s ambitious climb. Fans of lavish period pieces will find new tension as characters like Agnes van Rhijn and Peggy navigate heightened social stakes. The premiere arrives with a perfect 100 % Rotten Tomatoes score, underscoring critical confidence in the show’s direction. This launch not only continues the series’ Emmy‑nominated run but also signals a broader trend toward high‑budget historical dramas on streaming platforms.

## What happened
The Gilded Age season 4 drops on HBO Max on Sunday, November 1, extending the series’ 33‑episode run into its fourth year. The episode picks up after Bertha Russell (Carrie Coon) secures a lofty position in New York’s high society, only to face the personal costs of her relentless ambition.

New antagonists emerge, most notably Agnes van Rhijn, who is determined to reclaim influence lost to Bertha’s rise. Meanwhile, supporting characters such as Marian and Peggy confront their own uphill battles—Peggy struggles for acceptance from her future in‑laws, adding another layer of familial tension.

Early critical response is overwhelmingly positive, with the season holding a perfect 100 % score on Rotten Tomatoes and building on the franchise‑high eight Emmy nominations earned by season 3.

## Why it matters
The premiere underscores HBO’s commitment to high‑budget period storytelling, reinforcing the network’s reputation for prestige television. By deepening class conflict and expanding character arcs, the show invites viewers to reconsider historical power dynamics through a modern lens. Its critical acclaim also boosts HBO Max’s competitive edge in a streaming market saturated with contemporary dramas, positioning The Gilded Age as a flagship series that can attract both history buffs and binge‑watchers.
