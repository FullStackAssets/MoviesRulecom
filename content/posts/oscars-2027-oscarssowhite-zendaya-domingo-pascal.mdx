The Academy’s latest nomination forecasts suggest the 2027 Oscars could echo the 2015 #OscarsSoWhite controversy. Michael B. Jordan’s historic win last March raised hopes for broader representation, yet Gold Derby’s October projections list only three actors of color across all four acting categories. With nine of ten lead slots still occupied by white performers, the race appears to be narrowing before the ballot is even cast. If the pattern holds, the industry may face renewed criticism and pressure to overhaul its talent pipeline.

<Callout type="takeaway">The 2027 Oscars are on track for another predominantly white acting slate, despite recent breakthroughs.</Callout>

## What happened

In March, Michael B. Jordan became the sixth Black man in 98 years to win Best Actor for “Sinners,” a film that also set a record with 16 nominations. Eight months later, Gold Derby’s aggregated predictions for the upcoming ceremony show only three actors of color—Zendaya, Colman Domingo, and Pedro Pascal—among the projected nominees in the four acting categories, while no directors of color appear in the projected slate.

The USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative’s 2025 report shows that 41.5% of speaking or named characters in the year’s top‑grossing films came from underrepresented racial or ethnic groups, suggesting progress on‑screen. However, the same data reveal that behind‑the‑scenes roles such as writers, producers, and especially directors remain overwhelmingly white, creating an upstream bottleneck that limits diverse talent from reaching awards‑season visibility.

## Why it matters

When the Academy’s nominee list skews heavily white, it reinforces the perception that Hollywood’s most prestigious platform remains inaccessible to many creators of color. This not only undermines the industry’s public diversity pledges but also affects funding, marketing, and future casting decisions for underrepresented artists. A repeat of the 2015 backlash could spur stronger activist pressure, legislative scrutiny, and a possible reevaluation of the Academy’s voting and membership processes.

## How to think about it

Stakeholders should treat the early predictions as a diagnostic tool, not a verdict. Studios can prioritize attaching diverse talent to high‑budget, awards‑type projects well before the campaign season. Talent agencies should push for inclusive mentorship pipelines that move writers and directors of color into visible roles. Finally, voters need transparent criteria that reward not just on‑screen presence but also the breadth of contributions behind the camera.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the final nominee list will mirror early forecasts; last‑minute campaign pushes and surprise performances can still reshape the slate.</Callout>
