Riz Ahmed, the Oscar‑winning actor‑producer, has signed on as an executive producer for Elham Ehsas’ short documentary “Forgotten Spaceman.” The film follows Abdul Ahad Momand, Afghanistan’s lone astronaut, and has already collected awards at Locarno, Toronto, London’s LFF and Aesthetica. Ahmed’s involvement adds star power to a project that already counts Ken Loach’s Sixteen Films among its backers. The move highlights a growing interest in under‑told global stories within the festival circuit. It also positions the short for wider distribution ahead of its upcoming IDFA screening.

<Callout type="takeaway">Riz Ahmed’s executive‑producer role brings high‑profile attention to a pioneering Afghan space story.</Callout>

## What happened
Elham Ehsas’ short film “Forgotten Spaceman” dramatizes the 1988 Soviet mission that sent Afghan cosmonaut Abdul Ahad Momand into orbit. The film interweaves Momand’s personal journey with the broader context of Afghanistan’s turbulent history, drawing on interviews conducted by Ehsas’ father, the first journalist to speak with Momand after his return.

The short has screened at Locarno, Toronto, the London Film Festival and recently won Best Documentary at the Aesthetica Short Film Festival. It is now part of the competition lineup at the International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam, with a production team that includes Sixteen Films, Khan & Kumar Media and Prism Entertainment.

## Why it matters
By foregrounding Afghanistan’s lone astronaut, the film challenges the dominant narratives that reduce the country to conflict, offering a rare glimpse of scientific ambition and personal resilience. Ahmed’s attachment signals to investors and distributors that stories from under‑represented regions can attract mainstream talent and audiences. The growing festival momentum may translate into broader theatrical or streaming opportunities, amplifying Afghan cultural heritage on the world stage.
