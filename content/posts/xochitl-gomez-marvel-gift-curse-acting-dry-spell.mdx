When Xochitl Gomez stepped onto the screen as America Chavez in 2022’s *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness*, she instantly became a recognizable face in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. The role earned her critical praise and a high‑profile win on *Dancing with the Stars*, but it also introduced an unexpected hurdle. Gomez tells reporters that casting directors now assume she’s either too expensive or perpetually booked for Marvel projects. This perception has left her searching for indie work that matches her artistic ambitions. Understanding how a breakout role can become both a launchpad and a barrier is essential for anyone tracking talent trends in Hollywood.
<Callout type="takeaway">A marquee MCU role can elevate an actor’s brand while simultaneously narrowing future casting opportunities.</Callout>
## What happened
Gomez explained in a recent interview that after *Doctor Strange*, she “had a hard time getting roles” because producers believed she would be “too expensive” or unavailable due to potential Marvel commitments. She described the situation as “a gift and a curse,” wanting to explore “cool indie movies” but facing a hiring freeze rooted in assumptions about her schedule and cost.

Despite the slowdown, Gomez stayed visible by winning the 2023 season of *Dancing with the Stars*. However, her character America Chavez has not reappeared in any subsequent Marvel film or series, even though the ending of *Multiverse of Madness* hinted at future training at Kamar‑Taj. Rumors of a *Strange Academy* series linger, but no official project has materialized, and there’s been no confirmation of her involvement in upcoming MCU events like *Avengers: Doomsday* or *Secret Wars*.

Gomez noted that Marvel retains the rights to her image, leaving the door open for a surprise return, but the lack of concrete announcements underscores how franchise contracts can both protect and stall an actor’s broader career.
## Why it matters
The situation highlights a broader industry pattern where high‑profile franchise work can paradoxically limit an actor’s flexibility. Studios often label such talent as “expensive” or “busy,” discouraging independent filmmakers from casting them, which narrows the pool of creative opportunities. For audiences, this means fewer chances to see beloved actors in diverse roles, potentially stalling the evolution of their craft. For the industry, it raises questions about how talent agencies negotiate contracts that balance franchise loyalty with artistic growth.

## How to think about it
Actors and agents should treat a blockbuster credit as a bargaining chip, not a career endpoint. Negotiating clauses that guarantee a window for non‑franchise work can preserve creative momentum. Casting directors need to evaluate talent on current fit rather than perceived scheduling constraints. Fans can support an actor’s broader portfolio by seeking out and promoting their smaller‑scale projects, helping break the “too busy” narrative.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that a Marvel contract guarantees immediate future appearances; lack of confirmation often reflects strategic secrecy, not an actor’s willingness to work.</Callout>
