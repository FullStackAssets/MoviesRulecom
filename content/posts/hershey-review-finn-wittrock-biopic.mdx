Angel Studios’ new biopic ‘Hershey’ arrives on Thanksgiving, positioning itself as both a family‑friendly film and a brand showcase. Finn Wittrock steps into the shoes of Milton Hershey, tracing the candy magnate’s journey from a Mennonite farm to the chocolate empire that still dominates shelves. The screenplay, penned by Sharon Paul and Timothy Michael Hayes, follows a sanitized narrative that leans heavily on sweet nostalgia. For viewers hoping for a gritty exploration of American industrial history, the film’s glossy tone raises questions about its purpose.

<Callout type="takeaway">The movie feels more like an extended advertisement than a revealing biography.</Callout>

## What happened
The film opens with a lovingly lit close‑up of a Hershey’s Kiss, immediately signaling its dual purpose as a story and a product showcase. Produced in part by Hershey Entities and distributed by Angel Studios, the biopic chronicles Milton Hershey’s early life in a Pennsylvania Mennonite community, his apprenticeship with confectioner Joseph Royer, and his eventual pivot from caramels to milk chocolate after a German salesman’s warning.

In the second half, Hershey’s romance with Kitty (Alexandra Daddario) fuels the narrative while the plot highlights a pivotal bulk order from an English visitor that propels his Lancaster shop into national prominence. The screenplay stitches together these milestones with a tone that feels more celebratory than investigative, culminating in a tidy resolution that reinforces the brand’s legacy.

## Why it matters
By framing Hershey’s ascent as a feel‑good origin story, the film doubles as a marketing vehicle that reinforces brand loyalty during the holiday season. Its release on a streaming platform owned by Angel Studios illustrates a growing trend where corporate entities produce biopics that blur the line between entertainment and advertisement. For audiences, the movie shapes perception of an iconic American entrepreneur, potentially eclipsing more nuanced historical accounts.

## How to think about it
Approach the film as light, family‑friendly entertainment rather than a definitive historical documentary. Use it as a springboard to explore the real story of Milton Hershey through books or reputable articles, especially if you’re interested in the labor practices and business strategies of the era. For casual viewers, enjoy the visual nostalgia and performances, but keep expectations of critical insight in check.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t expect a deep, investigative look at Hershey’s life; the film prioritizes brand positivity over gritty truth.</Callout>
