A24’s three‑hour documentary “You Can See Everything” has surged ahead of the typical Halloween horror fare. The film closed its second weekend with a $27.1 million domestic total and a 14‑day cumulative of $52.7 million, outpacing Warner Bros.’ Clayface. The unexpected strength comes amid a sluggish holiday frame, with overall weekend grosses lagging behind previous years. Analysts see the doc’s meme‑driven buzz and viral costumes as key drivers, reshaping expectations for non‑genre releases during holiday windows.
<Callout type="takeaway">The documentary’s meme‑fuelled momentum turned it into the Halloween weekend’s surprise box‑office leader.</Callout>
## What happened
The documentary ended its second week with a $27.1 million domestic haul and a 14‑day cumulative of $52.7 million, while Warner Bros.’ Clayface posted $33.6 million in its first week. Industry trackers project a roughly –40% drop for “You Can See Everything” in its third weekend, compared with a –60% decline expected for the genre‑heavy Clayface.
Social media played a pivotal role: memes featuring Elizabeth Holmes and Nathan Fielder as Halloween costumes kept the title in daily conversations, translating into steady ticket sales despite rain forecasts for Los Angeles.
Historically, Halloween weekends favor horror and superhero releases; last year’s total was $57.3 million, the fifth‑lowest of 2025. This year’s slower frame made the documentary’s performance an outlier.
## Why it matters
The success proves that strong online buzz can override seasonal genre expectations, giving documentaries a viable path to mainstream box‑office relevance. A24’s willingness to give a three‑hour film a wide release signals confidence in audience appetite for long‑form nonfiction when paired with cultural moments. Studios may reconsider holiday windows for niche titles, balancing risk against the potential of viral amplification.

## How to think about it
Industry watchers should monitor social‑media trends early in a film’s lifecycle and weigh them against traditional genre performance data. When a niche title aligns with a cultural meme, a wider release—even during traditionally genre‑heavy weekends—can be justified. However, planners must also account for runtime and audience fatigue, pairing strong digital buzz with strategic theater counts to maximize profitability.
