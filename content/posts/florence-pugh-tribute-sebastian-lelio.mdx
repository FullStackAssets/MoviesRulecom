Florence Pugh took to social media Monday to mourn the sudden death of Chilean filmmaker Sebastián Lelio. The Oscar‑winning director, who collaborated with Pugh on the 2022 psychological drama \"The Wonder,\" died after health complications at age 52. In a heartfelt post, Pugh described her grief, recalling their shared love of food, dancing, and his dedication to women‑centered stories. The tribute shines a light on Lelio’s impact on contemporary cinema and the personal bonds that form behind the camera.

<Callout type="takeaway">Sebastián Lelio’s loss is felt both in the film world and in the personal lives of collaborators like Florence Pugh.</Callout>

## What happened
Sebastián Lelio was found dead in his home last week, with his partner Amalia Kassai confirming his passing was due to health complications. The director, 52, was best known for the Oscar‑winning "A Fantastic Woman" (2017) and his recent collaboration with Pugh on "The Wonder," a tense period drama exploring faith and illness.

Pugh’s Instagram tribute quoted her saying, \"My heart is broken.\" She recalled evenings of foodie dinners in Dublin, shared meals of meat, stews, cheese, and red wine, and the way Lelio encouraged her to dance, even if it was \"ugly but human dancing.\" The post highlighted his deep care for women’s stories and his vibrant personality.

Beyond "The Wonder," Lelio’s filmography includes critically acclaimed works that elevated Chilean cinema on the global stage, earning him a reputation as a storyteller who foregrounded marginalized voices.

## Why it matters
Lelio’s death removes a singular voice that championed nuanced, female‑centric narratives, leaving a gap in both Chilean and international film landscapes. His collaborations, like the one with Pugh, demonstrated how cross‑cultural partnerships can enrich storytelling and bring underrepresented perspectives to wider audiences. For actors and filmmakers, his commitment to authenticity and empathy serves as a model for future projects.
