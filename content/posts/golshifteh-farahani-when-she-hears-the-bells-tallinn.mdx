At the 30th Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival, Iranian star Golshifteh Farahani introduced her latest work, 'When She Hears the Bells,' a debut feature by director Chabname Zariab. The film follows Leila, a young widow in Kabul who confronts the brutal reality of bacha bazi and the looming Taliban threat. Farahani spoke to The Hollywood Reporter about the movie’s urgent call for human rights and how it bridges art and war. Her presence underscores the film’s global relevance and the courage of those fighting for freedom.

<Callout type="takeaway">The film’s premiere highlights the power of cinema to spotlight human rights struggles in conflict zones.</Callout>

## What happened

The world premiere took place on Wednesday night in the First Feature Competition, drawing a crowd of international critics and festival-goers. Zariab’s script, shot on location in Afghanistan, portrays Leila’s attempt to escape while rescuing boys from a bacha bazi house. The narrative blends thriller elements with intimate human drama, aiming to resonate with Western audiences.

During her interview, Farahani explained how she was initially hesitant but ultimately accepted the role after persistent encouragement from Zariab. She praised the script’s depth and the director’s vision, noting that the film’s themes of resilience and resistance are universal. Farahani also emphasized the importance of standing up for human rights, urging viewers to recognize the film’s message beyond entertainment.

## Why it matters

By foregrounding the plight of Afghan women and children, the film challenges Western audiences to confront the ongoing human rights crisis in the region. It also demonstrates how independent cinema can amplify marginalized voices, potentially influencing policy discussions and humanitarian aid. For Farahani, the project reaffirms her commitment to roles that carry social impact, reinforcing her standing as a politically conscious artist.
