Comcast and Skydance have confirmed that SkyShowtime, the European joint‑venture streaming service, will cease operations at the close of 2027. The decision follows a board‑level internal note informing staff that the service, launched in 2022 across 22 markets, never achieved profitability despite more than $1 billion in spend. CEO Monty Sarhan will leave the company, with CFO Mark Neil stepping in as interim general manager. The shutdown marks the end of a high‑profile attempt to blend Paramount+, Peacock, and local content under one brand. For subscribers and industry observers, the closure signals shifting strategies among legacy media conglomerates in a crowded streaming landscape.

<Callout type="takeaway">SkyShowtime’s shutdown underscores the difficulty of scaling a joint‑venture streamer in Europe without a clear profit path.</Callout>

## What happened
The board announced that SkyShowtime will wind down its operations by December 31, 2027. CFO Mark Neil will assume the role of interim general manager while CEO Monty Sarhan departs. Staff were notified through an internal memo this week.

SkyShowtime launched in 2022 as a joint venture between Comcast’s NBCUniversal and Paramount (then ViacomCBS) to serve 22 European territories lacking Paramount+ or Peacock. The service combined content from Paramount, Peacock, Sky Studios and local originals, backed by an investment of at least $1 billion.

Despite the sizable spend, the platform remained in the red and never turned a profit, prompting shareholders to pressure a strategic review that ultimately recommended winding down.

## Why it matters
The closure removes a niche but costly brand from the European streaming battlefield, forcing subscribers to migrate to competing services and leaving content‑rights negotiations in flux. For Comcast and Skydance, ending SkyShowtime frees capital to reinforce their core platforms—Peacock and Paramount+—and to pursue more focused regional partnerships. The move also signals to the industry that joint‑venture models must demonstrate clear paths to profitability amid intensifying competition from global players.

## How to think about it
When evaluating joint‑venture streaming projects, weigh the upfront content spend against realistic subscriber growth in each territory, and set clear profitability milestones. Prioritize flexible licensing deals that can be untangled if the partnership ends, and maintain a contingency plan for subscriber migration to avoid churn. Finally, align the venture’s brand strategy with the long‑term goals of each parent company to prevent strategic drift.
