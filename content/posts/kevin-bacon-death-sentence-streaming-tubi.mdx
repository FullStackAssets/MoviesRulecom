The 2007 revenge thriller *Death Sentence* starring Kevin Bacon has resurfaced as a streaming title on Tubi, nearly two decades after its theatrical run. James Wan, now known for blockbuster action and horror, directed the film early in his career, showcasing a raw, gritty style that predates his later hits. The movie follows a grieving father who takes the law into his own hands after his son is killed by a gang. Its arrival on a free ad‑supported platform gives both longtime fans and new viewers a chance to reassess a forgotten entry in the modern vigilante canon. As streaming libraries compete for niche titles, this move highlights how older genre pieces can find fresh relevance.

## What happened
James Wan’s directorial debut in the action arena came with *Death Sentence* in 2007, a film that built on the moral questions raised by the original *Death Wish* novel. The story centers on Nick Hume (Kevin Bacon), a mild‑mannered dad whose teenage son is murdered during a gang initiation, prompting Hume to embark on a violent quest for retribution. The film’s gritty choreography and unflinching look at the cycle of violence earned it a modest cult following, though it never achieved mainstream success.

In October 2026, Tubi announced that *Death Sentence* would join its catalog, available to stream at no cost to U.S. viewers. The addition comes as part of Tubi’s broader strategy to bolster its library with under‑the‑radar titles from the 2000s, aiming to attract viewers seeking action‑drama nostalgia.

## Why it matters
