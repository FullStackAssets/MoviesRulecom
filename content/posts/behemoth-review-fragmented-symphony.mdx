Behemoth! arrives as Tony Gilroy’s most audacious effort, placing Pedro Pascal’s Alex Sarian—a returning cello virtuoso—at the heart of a music‑saturated narrative. The film follows Alex as he navigates Los Angeles’ scoring studios, tangled relationships, and childhood trauma, all filtered through a constantly shifting soundtrack. Its structure mirrors a symphonic composition, with crescendos, false notes, and fragmented movements that keep the audience on edge. For viewers attuned to sound‑first storytelling, the payoff is immersive; for those seeking a straightforward plot, the experience can feel disorienting.

<Callout type="takeaway">Behemoth! is a daring, music‑driven experiment that rewards attentive viewers but can feel scattered.</Callout>

## What happened
Behemoth! opens with Alex Sarian, a seasoned cellist, returning to Los Angeles after two decades abroad to score Hollywood films. As he rehearses and records, the diegetic music begins to bleed into his memories, pulling the audience between present studio sessions and flashbacks of his prodigy childhood, volatile ex‑partner Carol, and a fleeting affair with violinist Nadia. The narrative unfolds in a non‑linear fashion, using musical motifs to signal shifts in time and emotional state.

Gilroy layers tonal inflections throughout, allowing scenes to function as movements within an overarching symphony. Alex’s interactions with his podcast‑obsessed brother, played by Will Arnett, and his struggle to adapt to a reshaped industry serve as counterpoints that heighten the film’s emotional dissonance. The result is a portrait that feels both fragmented and cohesive, as if the audience is listening to an elongated piece of music rather than watching a conventional story.

## Why it matters
Behemoth! challenges the dominant narrative model by treating sound as the primary storytelling engine, a move that could inspire future filmmakers to explore diegetic music more aggressively. Its willingness to sacrifice linear clarity for tonal resonance highlights a growing appetite among streaming audiences for experimental forms that demand active listening. The film also spotlights the evolving role of musicians within the Hollywood ecosystem, reflecting broader industry shifts toward hybrid creative identities.

## How to think about it
Approach Behemoth! as you would a symphony: let the recurring musical themes guide you through the fragmented scenes, and resist the urge to force a linear plot onto the experience. Take notes on how specific instruments signal emotional beats, and allow the film’s mood shifts to inform your interpretation of Alex’s journey. Viewing with headphones can amplify the diegetic cues and make the narrative’s rhythm clearer.

<Callout type="warning">Do not expect a conventional three‑act structure; the film’s purpose is to feel like a composition, not a textbook drama.</Callout>
