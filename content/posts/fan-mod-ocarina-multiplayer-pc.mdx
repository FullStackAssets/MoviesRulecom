A community‑driven mod has turned the newly released PC port of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time into a two‑player online co‑op experience. The mod, which decompiled the remake and added native 4K support with DLSS, lets two Links explore Hyrule together in real time. While the original 1998 title was strictly single‑player, this addition revives a long‑standing fan wish for shared adventure. Because the remake already boasts a 96 Metacritic score, the multiplayer layer could reshape how both newcomers and veterans engage with the classic.

<Callout type="takeaway">The fan‑made mod brings official‑grade co‑op to Ocarina of Time, but it remains unofficial and limited.</Callout>

## What happened
The community decompiled the unofficial PC port of Ocarina of Time, enabling native 4K resolution and DLSS performance boosts, then layered a two‑player online mode on top. In a short gameplay clip posted to Reddit, two Links can dodge‑roll, attack enemies, and navigate tunnels together, confirming functional co‑op.

The multiplayer feature is currently limited to two players and requires an online connection. It is not an official DLC; instead, it is an extension of the fan‑made port, illustrating how quickly the modding scene can add substantial content.

## Why it matters
Adding co‑op to a title long celebrated for its single‑player design reshapes the social experience of a classic, inviting friends to share the narrative. The mod showcases the power of community developers to extend modern ports, potentially influencing future official updates or inspiring similar projects. However, its unofficial status means users assume risk, and the limited player count may temper broader adoption.

Download the mod from a reputable community hub, scan it with antivirus software, and follow the installation instructions carefully. Expect occasional crashes or connectivity hiccups, and treat the experience as an experimental addition rather than a polished feature.
