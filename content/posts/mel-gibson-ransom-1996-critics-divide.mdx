The 1996 thriller Ransom, starring Mel Gibson as a desperate father, has resurfaced in streaming lineups with a surprisingly warm audience reception. While the film now holds a 72% Rotten Tomatoes score and is often listed among Gibson’s best work, early reviews branded it "pure poison" and "reprehensible." The clash between critics and viewers highlights how perceptions of violence and heroism can shift over time. As more platforms add the movie to their catalogs, new viewers are encountering the debate for the first time.

<Callout type="takeaway">Ransom’s polarizing legacy shows that a film’s reputation can evolve dramatically with audience re‑evaluation.</Callout>

## What happened
Ransom premiered in 1996 with Mel Gibson playing a wealthy corporate executive whose son is kidnapped, prompting him to take the law into his own hands. Contemporary critics such as the San Francisco Chronicle dismissed the film as "pure poison," condemning its glorification of violent retribution, while the Austin Chronicle called it "a reprehensible movie from just about every perspective." Over the decades, the movie has been re‑evaluated; it now carries a 72% rating on Rotten Tomatoes and is frequently cited among Gibson’s most exciting thrillers.

## Why it matters
The shift from critical scorn to cult appreciation illustrates how streaming services can revive overlooked titles and expose them to new audiences. For curators, Ransom offers a case study in balancing edgy content with viewer demand, while fans see a chance to reassess a film that challenges moral expectations. The ongoing debate also underscores the broader conversation about how violence is portrayed and justified in mainstream cinema.
