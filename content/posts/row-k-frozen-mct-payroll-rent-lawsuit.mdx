Row K Entertainment, the indie distributor launched by Media Capital Technologies (MCT) in 2025, has been placed on ice. MCT announced it is freezing all film distribution activities after Row K missed employee payroll, rent and health‑insurance payments in August and laid off four staff members. The move also comes as the company falls behind on settlement payments tied to confidential lawsuits and faces a $3 million claim from BANKEX. With staff forced to cover medical costs out of pocket, the shutdown threatens the fate of the two films already released and the broader U.S. theatrical distribution market. Industry observers see this as a warning sign for rapidly expanding boutique distributors.

<Callout type="takeaway">MCT’s freeze underscores how cash‑flow failures can instantly derail a film distributor’s operations.</Callout>

## What happened
MCT issued a statement saying it had to "freeze all new activities related to film distribution and significantly reduce operating expenses" while refocusing on its core business. The decision follows Row K’s failure to pay employee payroll and rent for August, the loss of health‑insurance coverage for remaining staff, and the laying off of four employees.

An insider confirmed that Row K also missed several settlement payments tied to confidential lawsuits settled earlier this year. The financial strain is compounded by a lawsuit filed by BANKEX in New York State Supreme Court seeking at least $3 million in damages for alleged unpaid obligations by Christopher Woodrow and his companies.

Row K entered the market with a splashy debut, acquiring four films at the Toronto International Film Festival and releasing two. Its January release, “Dead Man’s Wire,” earned less than $4 million worldwide, after which the remaining titles were offloaded.

## Why it matters
The freeze leaves Row K’s employees without wages and forced to pay for essential medical procedures, raising immediate labor concerns. Investors and partners now face heightened risk as the distributor’s ability to honor existing contracts is uncertain. For the independent film ecosystem, the collapse highlights the fragility of rapid expansion without solid cash reserves, potentially discouraging future boutique ventures.

## How to think about it
When assessing a boutique distributor, prioritize audited financial statements over hype, verify payroll and insurance compliance, and diversify distribution channels to mitigate single‑point failures. Stakeholders should also monitor legal exposures that can quickly drain resources.
