At the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival, a high‑profile panel titled “Women, War and Witness: Cinema in Times of Global Crisis” convened to confront Hollywood’s portrayal of women. Actors and filmmakers including Golshifteh Farahani, director Halkawt Mustafa, and producer Natalia Libet shared personal experiences of misrepresentation and the pressure to rewrite regional histories. The discussion, moderated by Egyptian critic Ahmed Shawky, highlighted the urgent need for authentic female voices as the world grapples with conflict and displacement. Organizers framed the debate as a litmus test for the industry’s willingness to move beyond stereotypical narratives.

<Callout type="takeaway">Hollywood must prioritize genuine female perspectives over profit‑driven historical rewrites.</Callout>

## What happened

The panel brought together Farahani, who argued that “Hollywood rewrites history” and that white‑supremacy‑driven studios dictate narratives, often at the expense of truth. She recounted her own frustration with scripts that reduced Iran to terrorist or underground party tropes, forcing her to reject roles that compromised her dignity.

Director Halkawt Mustafa discussed his Oscar‑contending film about a Kurdish sniper rescuing her sister, emphasizing the need for stories that reflect women’s agency in war zones. Producer Natalia Libet highlighted the work of 2Brave Productions, a Ukrainian women‑led company aiming to amplify regional voices amid ongoing conflict.

The session was part of the Industry@Tallinn & Baltic Event, a market platform within the 30th Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival, and served as a forum for industry leaders to address gendered storytelling during a global crisis.

## Why it matters

When Hollywood continues to reshape histories through a narrow lens, it reinforces cultural erasure and limits audience understanding of complex realities. Authentic female narratives can broaden market appeal, foster cross‑cultural empathy, and provide role models for under‑represented communities. Moreover, supporting women‑centered projects can diversify funding streams and reduce reliance on homogenized studio formulas, ultimately reshaping power dynamics within the global film economy.

## How to think about it

Filmmakers should seek co‑production partners who share a commitment to authentic representation and explore alternative financing such as grants, crowd‑funding, or regional film funds. Audiences can prioritize films that credit women in key creative roles and voice support on social platforms. Studios need to embed gender‑sensitivity checks into development pipelines and allocate budget slots specifically for women‑directed or women‑centered projects.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume a single panel will instantly overhaul entrenched studio practices; systemic change requires sustained advocacy and concrete policy shifts.</Callout>
