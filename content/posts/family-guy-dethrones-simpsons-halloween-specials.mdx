Family Guy’s annual Halloween specials have landed on Hulu, sparking talk that the show has finally eclipsed The Simpsons after a quarter‑century of rivalry. The Simpsons, now in its 38th season, has long set the benchmark for animated horror anthologies with its “Treehouse of Horror” series that began in season 2. This year, Family Guy not only matched the yearly tradition but, according to industry observers, delivered a fresher, more daring lineup that resonated with both teen and adult audiences. The shift highlights a broader evolution in animated comedy, where streaming platforms give creators more freedom to experiment. For fans and streaming watchers, the change could reshape how they prioritize binge‑watch lists each October.

<Callout type="takeaway">Family Guy’s Hulu Halloween specials have become the new must‑watch horror anthology, nudging The Simpsons into the runner‑up spot.</Callout>

## What happened
The Simpsons is currently airing its 38th season, and its “Treehouse of Horror” episodes have been an annual October fixture since season 2, influencing countless animated series that followed. In contrast, Family Guy launched its own Halloween anthology on Hulu, aligning its release schedule with the tradition while leveraging the streaming platform’s flexibility.

Critics note that the latest Family Guy specials feature tighter storytelling, sharper pop‑culture references, and higher production values than recent Simpsons entries, which some viewers feel have grown formulaic. This perceived edge has led many to claim that Family Guy is now delivering the superior Halloween experience.

## Why it matters
The shift signals a broader change in how animated comedy competes on streaming services, where on‑demand access and creative freedom can tip the cultural balance. Younger audiences, who favor binge‑ready content, are gravitating toward the fresher tone of Family Guy, potentially reshaping viewership metrics for legacy shows. Additionally, the rivalry underscores the importance of seasonal events in driving subscriber engagement for platforms like Hulu.
