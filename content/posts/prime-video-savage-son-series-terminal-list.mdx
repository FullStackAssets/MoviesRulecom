Prime Video just green‑lit a new drama titled “Savage Son,” extending the Terminal List universe that stars Chris Pratt as former Navy SEAL James Reece. The order follows the October debut of season two, which logged 830 million minutes of watch time in its first week. By adapting Jack Carr’s third novel, the streamer signals confidence in the franchise’s long‑term appeal. Fans will see Reece tackle his most personal mission yet, while the series promises a mix of military action and wilderness survival. The move also raises questions about the future of the original show and how the new installment will fit into the broader narrative.

<Callout type="takeaway">Savage Son marks Prime Video’s first expansion of the Terminal List storyline beyond the original series.</Callout>

## What happened
Amazon’s Prime Video announced a series order for Savage Son, a drama based on Jack Carr’s third book in the Terminal List novels. The story follows James Reece, played by Chris Pratt, as he confronts a dangerous, personal mission that pushes him into the wild.

The announcement arrived two weeks after season two of The Terminal List premiered on Oct. 21 and delivered 830 million minutes of viewing in the week of Oct. 19‑25. Production will be handled by Amazon MGM Studios, MRC, and Civic Center Media, though no writer or showrunner has been attached yet, and it remains unclear which, if any, of the original cast will reprise their roles.

## Why it matters
The expansion signals Amazon’s commitment to a franchise that already proved its pull, leveraging Pratt’s star power to retain subscribers and attract new ones. It also diversifies the narrative by moving from a conventional espionage thriller to a more personal, survival‑focused story, potentially broadening the audience. However, the lack of confirmed cast continuity and creative leadership introduces risk; fans may be hesitant to invest in a spin‑off that feels disconnected from the original tone.

## How to think about it
If you’re deciding whether to add Savage Son to your watchlist, consider how much you enjoyed the blend of tactical combat and character‑driven drama in season two. Treat the series as a companion piece rather than a direct continuation, and keep an eye on casting announcements for familiar faces. Finally, set expectations that the tone may shift toward wilderness survival, which could affect pacing.
