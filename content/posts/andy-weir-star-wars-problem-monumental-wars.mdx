Andy Weir, author of *The Martian* and *Project Hail Mary*, recently shared his take on the Star Wars franchise in an exclusive interview. He pinpointed a single structural flaw: the pattern of ending one galaxy‑spanning conflict only to launch another soon after. The comment came amid ongoing debates about the sequel trilogy, the recent *Mandalorian* series, and upcoming film projects. If the franchise continues this cycle, each victory may feel temporary, potentially eroding fan investment.

<Callout type="takeaway">Repeatedly resetting the stakes after every epic war threatens Star Wars’ narrative satisfaction.</Callout>

## What happened
Andy Weir, fresh off the success of the *Project Hail Mary* film adaptation and the upcoming Audible series *Exoplanet*, was asked what he would change if he ran the Star Wars franchise. He answered that the core problem is the franchise’s habit of concluding a monumental war only to immediately introduce a new one, leaving audiences with an unsatisfying sense that previous victories were fleeting.

The observation echoes long‑standing fan criticism of the sequel trilogy, especially the way *The Force Awakens* set up a new conflict after the original trilogy’s resolution. Recent entries such as *The Rise of Skywalker* and the *Mandalorian* have struggled to balance nostalgia with fresh stakes, and upcoming projects—including Simon Kinberg’s reported trilogy—are now being evaluated through the lens of Weir’s comment.

## Why it matters
When a saga repeatedly discards the consequences of a hard‑won victory, the emotional payoff for viewers diminishes. This pattern can lead to franchise fatigue, making each new installment feel like a rehash rather than a meaningful progression. Creators who ignore the issue risk alienating both legacy fans and newer audiences seeking lasting narrative stakes.

## How to think about it
For creators, consider a “reset‑pause” model: after a major conflict, allow a period of rebuilding that explores the aftermath before introducing the next threat. For fans, evaluate new releases based on how they honor past resolutions rather than merely delivering another showdown. This approach can preserve the saga’s epic scale while giving victories lasting significance.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume Weir’s critique is the sole issue; character arcs, diversity, and storytelling tone also play critical roles in the franchise’s health.</Callout>
