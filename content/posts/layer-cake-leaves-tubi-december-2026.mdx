Fans of Daniel Craig’s early work have just learned that his 2004 crime thriller ‘Layer Cake’ will disappear from Tubi’s free streaming catalog at the end of December 2026. The film, which helped showcase Craig’s gritty performance and paved the way for his casting as James Bond, has been available on the ad‑supported platform for over two years. Its removal means that viewers will lose a no‑cost way to revisit the movie that cemented Craig’s reputation as a serious actor. For streaming‑savvy audiences, the change highlights how licensing windows continue to shift even for cult‑favorite titles. Understanding the timeline and alternatives can help fans catch the film before it’s gone.

## What happened
The streaming service Tubi announced that the Daniel Craig‑led crime thriller “Layer Cake” will be removed from its free, ad‑supported library on December 31, 2026. The movie, originally released in 2004 and directed by Matthew Vaughn, stars Craig as a mid‑level drug dealer navigating a labyrinth of crime and betrayal. Tubi has not provided a specific reason for the pull, but the move aligns with typical licensing expirations that force platforms to rotate titles.

The removal comes at a time when Craig’s Bond legacy is back in the spotlight, with the franchise preparing for a new era under Denis Villeneuve. “Layer Cake” is often cited as the breakout role that convinced Bond producers of Craig’s suitability for the iconic spy, making its disappearance from a free service a notable loss for fans seeking to trace his career trajectory.

## Why it matters
The film’s exit from Tubi reduces the number of high‑profile, cost‑free titles available to cord‑cutters, potentially driving viewers toward paid services or rentals to watch the movie. For cinephiles, “Layer Cake” offers a rare glimpse of Craig’s pre‑Bond intensity and a stylistic bridge between British crime capers and modern action franchises. Its absence also underscores how streaming libraries are increasingly fluid, with even cult‑favorite titles subject to contractual churn.
