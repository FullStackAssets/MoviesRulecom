In 2008 the Royal Shakespeare Company revived Hamlet with a cast that instantly caught the attention of both theatre lovers and sci‑fi fans. Doctor Who’s Tenth Doctor, David Tennant, took the role of Prince Hamlet while his former co‑star Patrick Stewart appeared as the scheming King Claudius. Critics praised Tennant’s taut lead and Stewart’s chillingly composed villain, noting how the performance let Stewart’s Captain Picard demeanor turn into Shakespearean evil. The production was filmed, released on DVD in 2009 and now streams digitally, offering a rare blend of classic theatre and modern pop‑culture icons.

## What happened
The RSC’s 2008 Hamlet, directed by Gregory Doran, featured Tennant as Hamlet and Stewart as Claudius, creating a buzz that extended beyond the usual theatre audience.
Reviews highlighted Tennant’s “galvanizingly taut lead performance” and described Stewart’s Claudius as a “supremely composed, calculating killer,” noting the uncanny overlap with his Picard persona.
The production was recorded for television, issued on DVD in 2009 for $19.99, and is occasionally rebroadcast on PBS’s Great Performances, now also available for digital purchase.
## Why it matters
By pairing two of the most recognizable sci‑fi actors with Shakespeare’s most famous tragedy, the RSC attracted viewers who might never attend a stage production, expanding the audience for classical theatre. The crossover also demonstrates how iconic television personas can enrich a character’s interpretation, offering fresh analytical angles for scholars and fans alike. As streaming platforms continue to acquire theatrical recordings, this Hamlet serves as a template for how high‑profile casting can drive demand for recorded stage works.
