When a literary icon gets a fresh Netflix treatment, expectations run high. The streaming giant launched the eight‑episode miniseries “Ripley”, starring Andrew Scott as the titular con‑artist. By shifting the tone to black‑and‑white noir and aging the characters, the show aims to deepen the original’s themes of escapism and disillusionment. Its compact run invites binge‑watching, positioning the series as a standout entry in Netflix’s 2026 thriller slate.

<Callout type="takeaway">The series proves that a thoughtful re‑imagining can turn a classic novel into a binge‑ready, mood‑driven thriller.</Callout>

## What happened
Netflix released “Ripley” on November 15, 2026, delivering eight hour‑long episodes that trace the disappearance of Dickie Greenleaf (Johnny Flynn) in Italy, a job given to Tom Ripley (Andrew Scott) by his wealthy client Herbert Greenleaf (Kenneth Lonergan). The series, written and produced by Oscar‑nominee Steve Zaillian, opts for a stark black‑and‑white visual style and ages the characters roughly twenty years beyond Highsmith’s novel, allowing deeper exploration of their faded ambitions and the darker side of expatriate life. Supporting performances from Dakota Fanning as Marge Sherwood and Eliot Sum add texture, while the eight‑episode format lets the narrative linger on moral ambiguity that previous film adaptations skimmed.

## Why it matters
“Ripley” demonstrates how streaming platforms can revisit literary classics with enough creative liberty to feel fresh, while still honoring the source material’s core anxieties. The noir aesthetic and extended runtime cater to binge‑watchers seeking atmospheric depth, potentially setting a template for future adaptations that prioritize character study over fast‑paced action. For fans of the original novel or the 1999 film, the series offers a new lens on escapism, highlighting how age and disillusionment reshape ambition in a globalized world.

## How to think about it
Treat the series as a two‑night event: watch two episodes per evening, pause to note how each character’s disillusionment mirrors modern expatriate anxieties, and compare Scott’s Ripley to Matt Damon’s 1999 portrayal. Use the slower moments to appreciate the visual composition, then discuss the moral gray areas with fellow viewers to deepen the experience.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid marathon viewing in one sitting if you’re sensitive to the series’ bleak mood; take breaks to process the psychological tension.</Callout>
