Sky’s upcoming eight‑episode adaptation of Stieg Larsson’s The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo just expanded its Vanger family roster with two high‑profile actors. Game of Thrones veteran Jonathan Pryce will step into the role of patriarch Henrik Vanger, while James D’Arcy, known for his work in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, has been cast as Martin Vanger. The announcement also confirmed returning cast members such as David Oyelowo as Mikael Blomkvist and Viola Prettejohn as Lisbeth Salander. The new talent infusion could shape audience expectations and signal Sky’s ambition to compete in the premium streaming market.

<Callout type="takeaway">The addition of Game of Thrones and MCU talent elevates the series’ profile and raises the stakes for its reception.</Callout>

## What happened
Per Deadline, Sky has officially added Jonathan Pryce as Henrik Vanger, James D'Arcy as Martin Vanger, and Lena Olin as Isabella Vanger to the eight‑episode series. Alun Armstrong, Jessica Raine, Paul Ready, and Camille Cottin round out the Vanger family and newsroom roles, joining David Oyelowo as journalist Mikael Blomkvist and Viola Prettejohn as hacker Lisbeth Salander.

The adaptation reimagines Larsson’s 2005 novel for a present‑day setting, following Blomkvist’s investigation into the disappearance of a young woman tied to the powerful Vanger dynasty. Earlier screen versions include the 2009 Swedish film starring Noomi Rapace and the 2011 Hollywood version directed by David Fincher with Daniel Craig and Rooney Mara.

The casting choices draw on the actors’ genre credentials—Pryce’s High Sparrow in Game of Thrones and D’Arcy’s role as Edwin Jarvis in the MCU—suggesting a blend of dramatic gravitas and mainstream appeal for the new series.

## Why it matters
By attaching recognizable faces from two of television’s biggest franchises, Sky aims to attract both loyal fans of the source material and viewers who follow high‑profile actors. The Vanger family’s expanded cast promises deeper character dynamics, potentially differentiating the series from previous film adaptations. Success could reinforce Sky’s strategy to produce prestige‑level original content that competes with global streaming giants.
