When John Wayne rides off at the end of 1969’s True Grit, he delivers a line that has become a shorthand for the modern Western. The grizzled marshal Rooster Cogburn quips, "Come see a fat old man sometime," as he leaps a fence and disappears into the horizon. That off‑hand farewell contrasts sharply with the stoic, sunset‑riding exits that defined earlier cowboy heroes. Because the line captures both humor and vulnerability, it continues to influence how filmmakers and audiences view the genre.

<Callout type="takeaway">John Wayne’s final line redefined the Western hero by embracing imperfection.</Callout>

## What happened
The 1969 film True Grit, starring John Wayne as the unorthodox U.S. Marshal Rooster Cogburn, ends with Cogburn riding away after fulfilling his promise to the young Mattie. In that moment he jokes, “Come see a fat old man sometime,” a line that blends self‑deprecation with a playful challenge.

Unlike Charles Portis’s novel, which ends on a more subdued note, and the 2010 Coen Brothers remake that opts for a quieter departure, Wayne’s version injects levity and humanity into the archetype. Critics have highlighted the line as the film’s most memorable moment, noting how it subverts the traditional stoic cowboy exit and cements Wayne’s legacy as a cultural touchstone for the genre.

## Why it matters
The line’s blend of humor, vulnerability, and defiance reshapes the Western hero from an untouchable legend into a relatable, flawed figure. This shift opened the door for later filmmakers to explore more nuanced protagonists, influencing everything from gritty anti‑heroes to comedic westerns. Audiences now expect a degree of self‑awareness in the genre, making the line a benchmark for how Westerns balance myth and realism.

## How to think about it
When watching a Western, use Wayne’s line as a lens: ask whether the film lets its heroes show vulnerability or lean on self‑deprecating humor. Consider how the ending reflects the story’s tone—does it reinforce myth or humanize the protagonist? This framework helps you assess whether a modern Western is building on Wayne’s legacy or merely echoing it.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume this single line defines the entire genre; Westerns are shaped by many themes, styles, and cultural moments.</Callout>
