Peacock announced it will pull all eight Harry Potter films from its catalog just days before HBO rolls out its highly anticipated reboot series. The move aligns with HBO’s plan to launch the new adaptation on Christmas Day, beginning with The Philosopher’s Stone. By removing the legacy movies, the streaming landscape for the franchise is about to shift dramatically. Fans will need to navigate new subscription choices to keep up with both the classic films and the upcoming series.

<Callout type="takeaway">The removal of the Harry Potter films from Peacock clears the way for HBO’s reboot to become the franchise’s new streaming home.</Callout>

## What happened
In December 2026, Peacock will cease streaming all eight Harry Potter movies, a decision announced alongside HBO’s schedule for the new series. The reboot, executive‑produced by J.K. Rowling and showrunner Francesca Gardiner, is set to premiere on Christmas Day 2027, with each season adapting one of the original books.

HBO originally intended to release a new season annually, but insiders say the production timeline now spans roughly a decade. Rowling’s involvement, despite ongoing controversy over her trans‑rights statements, has been confirmed as a creative producer, joining director Mark Mylod on the project.

## Why it matters
Removing the films strips Peacock of one of its most recognizable franchises, potentially prompting subscriber churn as fans hunt for a place to rewatch the originals. At the same time, HBO gains an uncluttered launch window for its reboot, allowing the network to market the series without competing against the same content on another platform. The decision also reignites debate over Rowling’s participation, which could affect audience reception and brand perception for both services.
