During a post‑screening Q&A at the Academy Museum, Andrew Garfield spoke about his earlier admiration for OpenAI CEO Sam Altman while promoting the biopic 'Artificial'. He recalled Altman's congressional testimony and his apparent altruistic stance, describing the tech leader as “genuinely great” and a potential future best friend. Garfield’s comments shed light on how Hollywood figures interpret the personalities behind AI breakthroughs, a topic that resonates with both film enthusiasts and tech watchers.

<Callout type="takeaway">Garfield’s admiration underscores the cultural fascination with AI leaders and their portrayal on screen.</Callout>

## What happened
Garfield told the audience, “The first time I saw Sam, he was testifying in front of Congress and he was saying incredibly righteous stuff… He was talking about transparency; he was talking about the risks.” He added that Altman’s claim of having no equity in OpenAI and his expressed love for the technology made a strong impression on him.

He also noted Altman’s willingness to sit before Republican lawmakers and his practice of meditation with Buddhist teacher Jack Kornfield, saying, “This guy might be like my future best friend.” The remarks came after a screening of Luca Guadagnino’s ‘Artificial’, which dramatizes the founding of OpenAI and the brief ousting of Altman.

## Why it matters
Garfield’s praise highlights a broader narrative where tech innovators are cast as visionary protagonists, influencing public perception of AI development. When a high‑profile actor frames a CEO as altruistic, it can soften skepticism around powerful platforms like ChatGPT and shape audience expectations about corporate responsibility. The film’s release and the accompanying commentary therefore intersect with ongoing debates about regulation, transparency, and the societal impact of artificial intelligence.
