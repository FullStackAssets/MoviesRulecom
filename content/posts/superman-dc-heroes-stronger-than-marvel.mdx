Fans have been debating the power gap between DC and Marvel for years, and a recent Screen Rant piece brings a fresh angle by citing Superman’s own words. In the 1996 JLA/Avengers #2 crossover, Superman tells Thor that “the dials go up to eleven,” a line that has become shorthand for DC’s upper‑hand. The article outlines how the Justice League’s roster, from Wonder Woman to the Flash, routinely eclipses the Avengers’ mightiest members. It also points to the layered DC multiverse, whose abstract realms add extra narrative horsepower. Understanding this perspective matters for anyone tracking future crossover possibilities and cinematic adaptations.

<Callout type="takeaway">DC’s characters are written with inherently higher power ceilings than Marvel’s.</Callout>

## What happened
The Screen Rant story references the JLA/Avengers #2 comic where Thor’s hammer is caught by Superman, prompting the iconic reply about the “dials” being set to eleven. That moment illustrates a long‑standing editorial choice: DC often grants its heroes cosmic‑scale abilities, while Marvel tends to ground powers in relative human limits. The piece also highlights Marvel’s recent cinematic milestones—Thanos’ Snap, Scarlet Witch’s reality‑bending, and Thor’s god‑like strength—showing that even blockbuster feats sit below DC’s comic‑book extremes. Additionally, the article mentions the DC multiverse hierarchy, including the Sphere of the Gods and the Source Wall, which provides a built‑in framework for ever‑greater power levels.

## Why it matters
For fans, the power disparity influences how crossover stories are imagined; a Justice League versus Avengers showdown must account for Superman’s ability to out‑punch Thor. Creators and studios also use these power baselines when adapting characters for film and TV, often scaling down DC icons to fit realistic tones or amplifying Marvel heroes to meet audience expectations. The perception of “stronger” can affect merchandising, fan debates, and the strategic direction of future shared‑universe projects.

## How to think about it
When evaluating a DC or Marvel title, consider power level as one axis among tone, character motivation, and thematic depth. Ask whether the story uses a hero’s strength to explore larger ideas or merely to showcase spectacle. Balancing raw power with personal stakes often yields the most engaging narratives, regardless of which universe the character originates from.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t assume that higher power automatically guarantees a better or more compelling story.</Callout>
