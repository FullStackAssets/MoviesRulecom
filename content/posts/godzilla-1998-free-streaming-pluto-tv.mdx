Fans of the original monster mash have a new reason to revisit the 1998 Godzilla. After 28 years, the film has been added to Pluto TV’s free, ad‑supported catalog in the United States, with Fandango at Home doing the same on November 1. The timing coincides with the theatrical launch of Takashi Yamazaki’s Godzilla Minus Zero, giving audiences a chance to compare the two eras of the franchise. With no subscription fee, the re‑release could introduce the controversial reboot to a new generation. Streaming it now also fuels conversation ahead of the latest Japanese sequel’s box‑office run.

<Callout type="takeaway">The free streaming of Godzilla (1998) on Pluto TV serves as a low‑cost bridge between the franchise’s past and its newest sequel.</Callout>

## What happened
Collider reported that Godzilla (1998) is now available to stream for free with advertisements on Pluto TV in the U.S. The film also joined Fandango at Home’s free lineup on November 1, timed to the release of Godzilla Minus Zero.

The move arrives 28 years after the original’s theatrical run and follows the recent box‑office performance of Yamazaki’s sequel, which opened to an estimated $31.6 million in its second North American weekend.

## Why it matters
Offering the 1998 reboot at no cost lowers the barrier for casual viewers and can revive debate over its mixed legacy, while the simultaneous promotion of Godzilla Minus Zero helps drive awareness for the new Japanese installment. Streaming platforms benefit from ad revenue and increased traffic, and the franchise gains a unified marketing push across decades. For fans, the free access provides a convenient way to revisit a polarizing film before deciding whether to see the latest sequel in theaters.
