Daayra, Meghna Gulzar’s gritty police procedural, is finally arriving on Netflix on November 13. The film pairs Bollywood star Kareena Kapoor with Malayalam actor Prithviraj Sukumaran and will be streamed in both Hindi and Malayalam. Its OTT debut follows a theatrical run that earned mixed‑to‑positive reviews. By moving to a global platform, the movie reaches a wider audience for its hard‑hitting look at police encounters and institutional accountability. Viewers can now explore the story from their homes.

<Callout type="takeaway">Daayra’s Netflix launch expands the reach of socially conscious Indian cinema.</Callout>

## What happened
Netflix India South announced that Daayra will be available on November 13, in Hindi and Malayalam.
The story follows Ajooni (Kareena Kapoor), who heads an SIT investigating a controversial encounter killing tied to a gang‑rape and murder case, and DCP Raman Kumar (Prithviraj Sukumaran), the officer who led the encounter.
Hindustan Times critic Rishabh Suri gave the film three stars, calling the first half deliberate but praising the second half for raising questions about institutional accountability.

## Why it matters
The move to Netflix gives Daayra a far broader audience than its theatrical run, allowing the film’s critique of police accountability to spark conversation across regions. Streaming in both Hindi and Malayalam respects linguistic diversity and invites viewers from different markets to engage with the same social issue. For fans of socially driven cinema, the release demonstrates how OTT platforms can amplify stories that challenge institutional power.

## How to think about it
Approach Daayra as both a courtroom drama and a social commentary. Watch the first half for character setup, then allow the investigative momentum of the second half to guide your reflections on institutional justice. Consider pairing the viewing with discussions about real‑world encounter cases, and use subtitles if you’re not fluent in Hindi or Malayalam to capture nuanced dialogue.
