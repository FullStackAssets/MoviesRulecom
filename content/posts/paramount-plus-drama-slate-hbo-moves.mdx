The newly formed Skydance DTC unit is reshaping the future of Paramount+ drama content. Sources say the combined content team, led by Casey Bloys with Francesca Orsi and Sarah Aubrey, is evaluating whether several high‑profile Paramount+ series could debut on HBO. While no moves have been finalized, the discussion signals a potential realignment of flagship dramas across the two platforms. Industry watchers see this as a test case for the broader merger plan that aims to unite HBO Max and Paramount+ into a single service.

<Callout type="takeaway">Paramount+ dramas may appear on HBO, but any migration remains speculative.</Callout>

## What happened
Almost a month after Skydance’s acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, the new Direct‑to‑Consumer division placed the content operations of Paramount+ and HBO/HBO Max under Casey Bloys, who now serves as Co‑Chair and Chief Content Officer of Skydance DTC.

Bloys brought over senior HBO talent—including Francesca Orsi, Amy Gravitt and Sarah Aubrey—to oversee the combined slate, while Paramount+ EVP Jane Wiseman exited in the recent restructuring.

Internal sources say the team is reviewing Paramount+’s drama pipeline and has floated the idea of moving titles such as the legal thriller “Discretion,” the 9/11 limited series “The Best Of Us,” and other yet‑to‑greenlight projects to HBO, though no series have been officially relocated.

## Why it matters
If even a handful of Paramount+ dramas receive an HBO imprint, it could reshape audience expectations, boost subscription value, and set a precedent for how the eventual single streaming platform will allocate premium content. Creators may need to adjust storytelling approaches to fit HBO’s brand, while advertisers and investors watch for signals about the merged service’s competitive positioning against Netflix and Disney+.
