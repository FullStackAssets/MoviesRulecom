The Hellraiser franchise, long anchored by Doug Bradley’s iconic Pinhead, has seen two of its lesser‑celebrated sequels surge into streaming prominence. Over the weekend of October 16, 2026, both Hellraiser: Revelations (2011) and Hellraiser: Judgment (2018) landed in the Top 10 of Lionsgate+ in the UK, with Revelations at #7 and Judgment at #9. The surprise resurgence is amplified by the fact that both titles are available for free on Tubi in the United States, giving new audiences easy access. This unexpected boost highlights how legacy horror can find new life on digital platforms, even when original critical reception was lukewarm.

<Callout type="takeaway">The 2011 and 2018 Hellraiser sequels have become two of Lionsgate+ UK’s top‑10 streamed titles, reviving interest in the franchise.</Callout>

## What happened
The data released by Lionsgate+ shows that during the October 16 weekend, Hellraiser: Revelations secured the seventh spot while Hellraiser: Judgment held the ninth position on the service’s UK ranking. Both films, which originally received mixed to negative reviews, are now streaming for free on Tubi in the US, expanding their reach beyond the pay‑wall. Revelations follows two runaway friends whose encounter with the Lament Configuration drags their families into a nightmare, while Judgment adopts a police‑procedural angle, tracking a serial killer inspired by the Ten Commandments.

The surge aligns with a broader trend of horror libraries being refreshed on streaming services, where niche titles can climb charts based on binge‑watching patterns and algorithmic promotion. While Judgment earned slightly better critical notes than Revelations, neither film was a critical darling upon release; their current popularity stems largely from brand recognition and the accessibility of free streaming.

## Why it matters
The revival of these non‑Bradley sequels signals that streaming platforms can rehabilitate films that underperformed theatrically, turning them into cultural touchstones for new viewers. For Lionsgate+, the success demonstrates the value of leveraging established horror IPs to drive engagement, especially in competitive markets like the UK. For the Hellraiser franchise, the renewed visibility may influence future production decisions, potentially encouraging studios to explore fresh narratives or reboots that acknowledge the franchise’s broader catalog. Fans benefit from easy access, while the industry watches how data‑driven streaming performance can reshape legacy content strategies.

## How to think about it
If you’re a horror fan, treat these sequels as entry points to the broader Hellraiser mythos rather than definitive Pinhead experiences. Watch them to gauge how the franchise evolved post‑Bradley, then decide if you want to explore the original 1987 film or newer reboots. For creators, the case study underscores the importance of making legacy titles readily available on ad‑supported platforms, as accessibility can translate into measurable viewership spikes even for previously panned movies.

<Callout type="warning">Don’t assume the streaming popularity reflects high artistic merit; the films remain uneven and may not satisfy purists seeking the classic Pinhead atmosphere.</Callout>
