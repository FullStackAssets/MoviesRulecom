At DOC NYC, director Miguel Eek premiered his latest documentary Dryads, which follows three indie musicians as they explore how their surroundings shape their sound. The film delves into the creative process of a Sámi joik singer, a Spanish cave‑explorer, and a Portuguese re‑imaginator of folk tunes. By weaving field recordings with intimate portraits, Eek highlights the link between place, tradition, and innovation. The premiere underscores the festival’s role in spotlighting global voices that examine the origins of art. For music lovers and cultural scholars alike, Dryads offers a fresh lens on how environment fuels creativity.
<Callout type="takeaway">The film shows that music’s birth is rooted in place and tradition, revealing how diverse cultures turn surroundings into sound.</Callout>
## What happened
Dryads, produced by Mallorca‑based Mosaic, follows three musicians: Vasco Ribeiro of Portugal, Joana Gomila of Mallorca, and Norwegian Sámi artist Emil Karlsen. Each artist sources inspiration from local landscapes—Ribeiro reworks Portuguese folk, Gomila records in caves and abandoned buildings, and Karlsen draws on fjords and tundra for his joiks.
Eek’s observational style captures the musicians’ rehearsal spaces, field recordings, and moments of quiet reflection, emphasizing the improvisational nature of creation. The film premiered at DOC NYC’s international opening, drawing attention from critics who praised its intimate portrayal of cultural memory and sonic experimentation.
## Why it matters
Dryads shines a light on the often overlooked role of environment in artistic production, suggesting that place can be as much a collaborator as a muse. By foregrounding the stories of musicians from disparate backgrounds, the documentary challenges the notion that creativity is purely individual.
The film also invites audiences to reconsider how cultural heritage is preserved and transformed. For communities like the Sámi, whose joiks risk marginalization, the documentary offers visibility and a platform to share their evolving practices with a global audience.

## How to think about it
When watching Dryads, consider the three lenses the film presents: place, tradition, and improvisation. Ask yourself how each musician’s surroundings shape the timbre, rhythm, and emotional content of their work.
Use the film as a case study for analyzing how cultural context can inform creative practice, and apply that framework to your own projects by mapping local resources to artistic goals.
<Callout type="warning">If you prefer fast‑paced documentaries, Dryads’ contemplative style may feel sluggish.</Callout>
