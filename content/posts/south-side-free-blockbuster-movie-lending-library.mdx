A bright blue-and-yellow box on Jane Street has become an unexpected movie hub for South Side residents. The box, dubbed “Free Blockbuster,” is stocked with DVDs and VHS tapes that neighbors can take or leave behind. It was created by 49‑year‑old movie enthusiast Kathi Radock, who transformed an old newspaper dispenser in April 2023. In a city where Blockbuster stores vanished in 2014, the tiny library revives a tactile sharing culture. Its growing popularity shows how low‑tech community projects can thrive alongside streaming services.

<Callout type="takeaway">Free Blockbuster proves a repurposed box can reignite neighborhood movie sharing.</Callout>

## What happened
The concept traces back to 2019 when a former Blockbuster employee set up the first “Free Blockbuster” in Los Angeles, using a discarded newspaper dispenser to house VHS tapes and DVDs for anyone to borrow.
Inspired by that model, Radock sourced an old Pittsburgh newspaper box, painted it in Blockbuster’s iconic blue and yellow, and placed it on her South Side property in April 2023. The box now sits on Jane Street near the area locals call “The Hollah,” drawing passersby who stop to browse, take a film, or leave a title.
Since its launch, the box has attracted both kids and adults, sparked spontaneous photo‑ops, and even endured a graffiti tag that Radock left visible as a badge of its lived‑in character.

## Why it matters
Physical media libraries like Free Blockbuster offer a tactile alternative to algorithm‑driven streaming, fostering serendipitous discovery and a sense of ownership that digital playlists can’t replicate. They also create micro‑public spaces where neighbors interact, reinforcing community bonds in an era of isolated viewing. Moreover, by keeping DVDs and VHS tapes in circulation, the project helps preserve film history that might otherwise be discarded as streaming catalogs churn.

## How to think about it
If you’re considering a similar pop‑up library, start by locating a sturdy, weather‑proof container and securing permission from the property owner. Stock it with a mix of popular titles and hidden gems, label each item clearly, and set simple “take‑one‑leave‑one” rules. Promote the box with a small sign or social‑media post, and check it regularly to restock and address any wear. By keeping the process informal yet organized, you can nurture a sustainable sharing hub without heavy overhead.

<Callout type="warning">Make sure to verify local ordinances and obtain any required permits before installing a public lending box.</Callout>
