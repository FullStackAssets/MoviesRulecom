Variety’s annual “10 Actors to Watch” panel took center stage at the Newport Beach Film Festival on Oct. 18, offering a rare glimpse into the next generation of talent. The conversation, moderated by Variety in partnership with the festival, featured ten emerging performers—from Jaafar Jackson channeling his legendary uncle to indie stand‑outs like Esmé Creed‑Miles. Each actor discussed the challenges of inhabiting dark or complex roles and the confidence they’re gaining on screen. For audiences and industry watchers, the insights signal which voices may shape the coming awards season and streaming line‑ups.

<Callout type="takeaway">The panel shows that today’s rising actors are using bold, layered characters to fast‑track their credibility.</Callout>

## What happened
The panel kicked off at the Balboa Bay Resort, where Variety introduced the cohort: Jaafar Jackson (“Michael”), Inde Navarrette (“Obsession”), Ray Nicholson (“The Mosquito Bowl”), Esmé Creed‑Miles (“Sense and Sensibility”), Mariana Di Girólamo (“Wild Horse Nine”), Rosalind Eleazar (“Misty Green”), Guitarricadelafuente (“La Bola Negra”), Summer H. Howell (“Carrie”), Ruth Madeley (“Being Heumann”) and Joseph Zada (“The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping”). Moderators guided a discussion about performance craft, role preparation, and the personal stakes of tackling darker material.
Navarrette spoke about the difficulty of portraying Nikki in “Obsession,” noting that the scarcity of source material gave her creative freedom to explore the character’s complexity. Jackson reflected on embodying his uncle Michael Jackson, emphasizing the need to project confidence and avoid being labeled a novelty act. Other panelists highlighted how immersive research and collaborative support helped them transition from indie projects to higher‑profile productions.

## Why it matters
The conversation underscores a shift toward more nuanced, risk‑taking roles for emerging talent, a trend that streaming services are eager to capitalize on. By spotlighting actors who are already engaging with challenging material, the industry gains early indicators of performers who can attract audiences seeking depth over star power. Moreover, the panel’s emphasis on confidence building hints at a broader push for diverse representation behind and in front of the camera.
