Subscribers have refilled a federal lawsuit challenging Skydance’s $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount, focusing on a planned HBO Max‑Paramount+ bundle slated for early 2027. The complaint argues that the combined service would give the merged owner too much control over streaming, news and theatrical releases. Plaintiffs claim the bundle could force higher subscription costs and limit programming choices for consumers. The case arrives after two prior dismissals, making this filing the last chance to amend the suit.
<Callout type="takeaway">The refiling seeks to block a combined HBO Max‑Paramount+ service that could curb competition and raise costs.</Callout>
## What happened
On Thursday, a group of consumers filed a new complaint in California federal court, reasserting claims that the Skydance‑Warner‑Paramount megadeal consolidates excessive market power. The suit highlights the upcoming bundle of HBO Max and Paramount+ scheduled for early 2027, the ouster of DC Studios heads, and projected $6 billion in annual savings through operational consolidation.
According to the filing, while the two services may retain separate branding, they will answer to a single owner whose financial interests prioritize the combined slate. The plaintiffs argue that this arrangement eliminates price competition, reduces programming diversity, and threatens consumer choice across streaming, national news, and first‑run theatrical production.
## Why it matters
If the bundle proceeds, it could reshape the streaming landscape by concentrating two major platforms under one roof, potentially diminishing price competition and limiting the variety of content available to viewers. Consumers may face higher aggregate subscription fees, while smaller competitors could lose leverage in negotiations for talent and licensing. The outcome also signals how aggressively antitrust regulators will intervene in future media consolidations.

## How to think about it
Subscribers should monitor pricing announcements from both platforms and compare them with alternative services such as Netflix, Disney+ and Amazon Prime Video. If the bundle materializes, consider whether the combined offering meets your content needs or if switching to a competitor could preserve choice and cost efficiency. Keep an eye on antitrust developments, as a court injunction could preserve the status quo or force a restructuring of the bundle.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the bundle will be blocked; the lawsuit’s outcome remains uncertain and could still proceed.</Callout>
